# Primary interview programme

**Current evidence:** Public interviews, conference panels, podcasts, survey research and company disclosures.

**Next evidence gate:** Fourteen structured conversations across the publisher-advertiser transaction and the proposed vertical.

## Why state the gap this bluntly?

Public practitioner material establishes that CPA, CPC, CPL, tenancy, fixed fees and hybrid arrangements all exist. It does not establish what a named robotic-mower manufacturer, greenhouse retailer, awning installer or specialist publisher would agree with this new business.

Writing “industry interviews show” without distinguishing a public podcast from an interview conducted for this project would be false. This programme turns the missing primary evidence into a bounded research phase.

No one should be contacted until outreach is explicitly authorised. The programme below defines whom to recruit, what to ask, what evidence to retain and what decisions the answers should change.

## What must the interviews decide?

The interviews need to answer six commercial questions:

1. Will serious merchants work with a new specialist comparison publisher?
2. What event will they pay for: click, lead, sale, placement, content or a mixture?
3. What margin, attribution and internal budget constraints set the price?
4. What data and reporting make a publisher valuable enough to receive direct terms?
5. What makes the buyer trust an aggregator rather than a retailer or generic review site?
6. Which operational problem is painful enough to become software, data or subscription revenue later?

The conversations are not a referendum on whether the interviewee likes the idea. They are a search for current processes, real constraints, historical behaviour and contractual evidence.

## Who should be interviewed?

Fourteen conversations are enough to expose repeated objections without pretending to be statistically representative.

| Specialist content publisher       |     2 | Runs commerce content in home; garden; tools; leisure or maker equipment     | Editorial cost; merchant access; CPA and tenancy negotiation              |
| Comparison or CSS publisher        |     2 | Bills or earns merchant CPC and manages feeds                                | Click pricing; invalid traffic; merchant conversion feedback              |
| High-ticket advertiser             |     2 | Affiliate or ecommerce lead at a manufacturer or specialist retailer         | Product-level CPA; margin; attribution; feed quality                      |
| Installed-project seller           |     2 | Awning; garden-room; cabin or landscaping operator buying or receiving leads | Lead qualification; postcode capacity; accepted event and price           |

Recruit at least one small organisation and one scaled organisation on each commercial side. Large operators reveal mature processes; small operators reveal whether the model can work before scale.

## What should qualify a participant?

### Publisher

- Has personally negotiated or operated affiliate relationships in the last twelve months.
- Can distinguish tracked commission from approved and paid revenue.
- Has exposure to at least two payment models or can explain why only one is used.
- Works with high-consideration products or complex buying content.

### Advertiser

- Owns or materially influences affiliate; partnership; ecommerce; acquisition or trade-marketing budget.
- Can discuss product margin and targets at least in ranges.
- Has approved or rejected publishers.
- Has experienced tracking; validation; paid-placement or feed decisions.

### Installer or dealer

- Currently accepts enquiries from outside its own direct website.
- Can define its service area and capacity.
- Knows approximate enquiry-to-survey and survey-to-sale performance.
- Has authority to discuss commercial lead arrangements.

### Buyer

- Completed a purchase or reached a real shortlist.
- Can reconstruct the journey using browser history; emails; saved products or quotes where comfortable.
- Is not being asked to imagine future behaviour in the abstract.

## How should each interview be run?

- 35–45 minutes.
- Ask permission before recording.
- Use notes if recording is declined.
- Do not send the full hypothesis before the call; avoid training the participant to validate it.
- Ask for specific recent examples before opinions.
- Separate confidential evidence from publishable statements.
- End by asking what evidence would change their own view.
- Do not promise coverage, leads, rankings or commercial terms during research.

The interviewer should record the participant's seat and decision authority rather than inflate a title into expertise.

## Publisher interview guide

### Current model

1. Walk me through the last product-comparison or commerce article you monetised.
2. Which parties were involved from product selection to payment?
3. What was the contracted event: click; lead; sale; placement or something else?
4. Which revenue number do you trust operationally: tracked; approved; invoiced or paid?
5. How long did it take from publishing to cash receipt?

### Economics

6. What production work could not be supported by CPA alone?
7. When do you ask for a fixed fee or tenancy?
8. When would you accept CPC instead of CPA?
9. How do you price a placement without knowing future conversion?
10. How do you evaluate whether a higher commission is better than a fixed payment?
11. Which advertiser costs or data are normally hidden from you?

### Commercial operations

12. What gets a new publisher approved or rejected?
13. What does a useful media kit show?
14. Who controls the budget for tenancy versus affiliate commission?
15. Which contractual clause causes the most trouble?
16. How often do rate; feed; tracking or programme changes damage existing content?
17. What causes a merchant to become commercially deprioritised?

### Product and trust

18. What original evidence most improves conversion?
19. What makes merchant data unusable?
20. Where must commercial and editorial decisions be separated?
21. How have search and AI summaries changed the acquisition mix?
22. Which first-party audience or product would you build earlier if starting again?

## Comparison and CPC publisher interview guide

1. What is a billable click and which clicks receive credits?
2. Are merchants charged one rate; category rates; auction rates or performance-adjusted rates?
3. What minimum volume makes a direct merchant CPC contract worth operating?
4. How are device; country; duplicate; bot and rapid-repeat clicks handled?
5. Does the merchant share downstream sales or target cost of sale?
6. What happens when merchant conversion falls?
7. How do feed errors affect billing disputes?
8. Can commercial payment affect ranking or only separate sponsored inventory?
9. What merchant reporting drives retention?
10. How much working capital and bad-debt exposure does direct CPC create?
11. What would prevent a new specialist comparison service from being accepted?

The important output is not a single “typical CPC.” It is the pricing mechanism and quality contract.

## Advertiser interview guide

### Objectives and budgets

1. What was the last affiliate or commerce-publisher partnership you approved?
2. Which commercial objective funded it: direct sales; new customers; stock movement; launch; awareness; content or data?
3. Which team owned the money?
4. Was the commercial model CPA; CPC; CPL; tenancy; fixed production or hybrid?
5. What did you need to believe before approval?

### Unit economics

6. What target cost of sale or acquisition range governs the category?
7. Can you share gross-margin or allowable-marketing-cost bands rather than exact margin?
8. Do targets change by product; new customer; bundle; season or stock position?
9. What order elements are non-commissionable?
10. How do returns; cancellations and finance affect approval?
11. What makes a click valuable enough to pay for before a sale?

### Publisher selection

12. Why have you rejected specialist publishers?
13. What minimum audience or sales history matters?
14. Does expertise compensate for small scale?
15. Which traffic methods are prohibited?
16. What content or comparison data would make this publisher distinctive?
17. What reporting do you wish publishers supplied?

### Tenancy and content

18. When do you buy tenancy or paid placement?
19. What is guaranteed and which outcome remains a target?
20. How do you calculate blended CPA after a fixed payment?
21. Would you trade an exclusive price; bundle; product access or enhanced CPA for placement?
22. Who owns product samples and content rights?
23. How is sponsored visibility kept separate from editorial ranking?

## Installed-project seller interview guide

1. Describe the last ten enquiries you received and which were actually useful.
2. What information is required before the sales team should call?
3. Which postcodes and project values are serviceable?
4. What is current survey capacity?
5. Which event would you pay for: accepted details; connected call; booked survey; attended survey or sale?
6. What are the recent conversion ranges between those stages?
7. Which lead rejection reasons are legitimate?
8. How should duplicates and existing customers be handled?
9. Do you want exclusive or shared leads?
10. How quickly can the team respond?
11. What proof of consent is required?
12. Would a fixed territory commitment improve planning?
13. What project data could the publisher return that would improve quoting or product design?

Ask for funnel ranges only when the participant is authorised to share them. Record “not disclosed” rather than reverse-engineering from anecdotes.

## Marketplace or dealer interview guide

1. Is the primary constraint buyer demand; suitable stock; listing quality or response time?
2. How do dealers currently distribute inventory?
3. What is paid monthly and what is paid per result?
4. Which tools justify a recurring subscription beyond simple listings?
5. What enquiry data predicts a sale?
6. How are old or sold listings removed?
7. What condition; hours; service and attachment data must be normalised?
8. Who owns photography and listing content?
9. Would dealers provide transaction outcomes in exchange for better reporting?
10. Which financing; inspection and transport referrals are commercially material?

## Specialist creator interview guide

1. Which buyer questions recur before a high-ticket purchase?
2. What does a credible test require in time; access; travel and equipment?
3. What would make you reject a brand-funded test?
4. Do you prefer production fee; CPA; fixed sponsorship or hybrid compensation?
5. How long are you willing to wait for commission validation?
6. Which claims require hands-on evidence?
7. What content rights can a brand buy and which remain yours?
8. Would you publish within another platform or only on your own channels?
9. What audience data would you share?
10. What would make a creator collection useful rather than a generic storefront?

## Buyer interview guide

Use a reconstruction rather than a concept pitch.

1. What triggered the purchase?
2. What did you search first?
3. Which websites; videos; shops and people influenced you?
4. Show me the shortlist or explain which products were eliminated.
5. Which specification was hardest to understand?
6. Which costs appeared late?
7. What made a source trustworthy or untrustworthy?
8. Did you move between phone; computer; showroom and calls?
9. Did you click back to a retailer later or search for the brand separately?
10. What nearly stopped the purchase?
11. Which comparison would have saved the most time?
12. Would you have saved a project; requested a shortlist review; or compared installers?
13. What would you pay for and what must remain free?

Do not ask “Would you use an aggregator like this?” until the end. Hypothetical approval is weak evidence.

## What commercial artifacts should participants be asked to show?

Where participants are comfortable and authorised:

- an anonymised programme term or insertion order;
- a media-kit page;
- a commission and validation report;
- rejection reason categories;
- payment timeline;
- merchant feed sample;
- lead acceptance rules;
- a placement report;
- product-level commission groups;
- an anonymised category P&L or target cost-of-sale band;
- a buyer shortlist or quote comparison.

Retain only evidence that the participant has permitted for the stated use. Record a structured fact, its source class and its disclosure boundary rather than collecting unnecessary confidential files.

## How should interview evidence be recorded?

Each material answer should become one evidence row:

```text
interview_id
participant_seat
organisation_type
decision_authority
interview_date
claim_or_observation
evidence_class
supporting_example
commercial_model
geography
publishable
attribution_allowed
limitations
```

Use these evidence classes:

- `observed-document`: an actual report; term or screen was shown;
- `reported-current`: participant describes a current process they own;
- `reported-historical`: participant describes a past example;
- `opinion`: interpretation or preference;
- `unknown`: the participant cannot establish it.

One confident speaker is not a market statistic.

## How should conflicts be handled?

Expected conflicts are useful:

- Publishers may say conversion risk sits with the merchant; advertisers may say traffic quality sits with the publisher.
- Advertisers may describe a programme as performance-only while agencies know paid placements are funded elsewhere.
- Networks may report tracking as operational while publishers observe missing sales.
- Installers may say they need more leads while their response time shows a capacity problem.
- Buyers may say price dominates while their actions show trust; delivery or service drove the decision.

Record both claims and seek a shared operational measure. Do not average incompatible definitions.

## What numbers should be requested as ranges?

- Publisher outbound click-through.
- Merchant conversion.
- Validation and rejection rate.
- Days from transaction to approval and cash.
- Gross-margin band.
- Target cost of sale.
- Tenancy budget and minimum audience.
- Lead-to-survey and survey-to-sale rate.
- Lead rejection and duplicate rate.
- Dealer monthly subscription tolerance.
- Production cost per original review or video.
- Revenue concentration by merchant.

Ranges are often sufficient for scenario modelling and easier to disclose than exact confidential figures.

## What would change the recommended business model?

### Evidence for a stronger CPA-first model

- At least three merchants offer usable feeds and 5% or better effective commission on target products.
- Tracking survives test purchases and cross-session journeys.
- Median approval and funding time is operationally tolerable.
- Merchant conversion makes approved revenue per qualified visit viable.

### Evidence for an earlier CPC product

- Merchants state they will pay for qualified outbound traffic.
- At least two agree a billable-click definition and downstream feedback.
- Organic or direct traffic can produce positive contribution without paid-search arbitrage.
- Click-quality and invoice controls can be implemented cheaply.

### Evidence for an earlier tenancy product

- Advertisers have separate paid-placement budget.
- They value the category audience before a large last-click history exists.
- The site can guarantee scarce inventory and report delivery.
- Fixed money funds original work rather than buying editorial conclusions.

### Evidence for a lead-led garden business

- Installers have capacity and clear territories.
- Accepted specification or booked survey can be defined consistently.
- Seller economics support a fee materially above acquisition and qualification cost.
- Disputes and consent can be governed.

### Evidence for choosing a different vertical

- Garden merchants reject the site or target categories are broadly non-commissionable.
- Installer lead economics do not support qualification cost.
- Maker or games-room merchants provide materially better access; tracking and product-level economics.
- Buyer interviews reveal a stronger unresolved decision problem elsewhere.

## What is the stopping rule for interview research?

Stop after fourteen completed conversations if:

- every seat has been represented;
- the last three conversations add no new material objection or payment mechanism;
- at least two counterparties independently support the proposed launch transaction;
- the major disagreements are documented rather than hidden;
- enough ranges exist to replace the largest economic assumptions.

Continue only for a named unresolved decision—for example whether installers prefer booked-survey or accepted-lead billing. Do not continue interviewing indefinitely to collect agreeable quotations.

## What should be produced after the interviews?

1. Updated evidence ledger with redacted and attributable versions.
2. Revised revenue model using reported ranges.
3. Merchant acceptance and payment-model matrix.
4. Buyer journey map based on reconstructed purchases.
5. A precise first commercial offer and insertion-order template.
6. A go; conditional-go; or stop decision for premium garden systems.
7. A record of which evidence contradicted the current thesis.

Until that work is done, the revenue architecture in this series is a disciplined commercial hypothesis—not a merchant-validated deal book.
