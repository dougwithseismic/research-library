# Industry evidence: publishers advertisers networks and public interviews

**Research date:** 3 September 2026  
**Geographic priority:** United Kingdom  
**Evidence status:** Public research and attributed practitioner testimony

## What evidence does this chapter add?

The wider report establishes search demand, order-value evidence, candidate verticals, partner programmes and a proposed product architecture. This chapter examines how publishers and advertisers negotiate value, how the UK affiliate market is paid, and how mature comparison and specialist-media businesses diversify revenue.

It uses four evidence layers:

1. UK network and platform transaction data from the Affiliate & Partner Marketing Association.
2. A multinational survey of marketers, publishers and creators from impact.com.
3. Public-company accounts showing how established publishers and marketplaces recognise revenue.
4. Public interviews, podcasts and video panels involving people working for publishers, advertisers, agencies, networks and tracking providers.

Public interviews are not equivalent to confidential primary research. Speakers choose what they disclose, hosts may have commercial incentives and case studies tend to select successes. They are nevertheless more useful than generic affiliate-marketing articles because named practitioners describe specific tensions: last-click attribution, fixed placement fees, product-level commissioning, payment delays, tracking failure and editorial value.

## What does the current UK affiliate market actually look like?

The APMA's 2026 State of the Affiliate Nation report uses 2025 submissions from 11 networks and platforms operating in the UK. It reports:

- £1.8 billion of advertiser investment;
- £20.7 billion of tracked advertiser revenue;
- 357 million transactions;
- 14.8 billion affiliate clicks;
- 7.3% year-on-year investment growth.

The report says two contributor datasets were partial and required some modelling. Amazon Associates did not submit data and was estimated from public information and expert discussion. Direct publisher-advertiser arrangements are difficult for networks to observe and are mostly outside the total. Lead-generation and several other excluded programme types mean the headline should not be treated as a complete census of every commercial partnership in Britain. [APMA 2026 report hub](https://theapma.co.uk/state-of-the-affiliate-nation-hub-non-member/), [APMA findings article](https://theapma.co.uk/uk-affiliate-and-partner-marketing-spend-surges-to-1-8bn-as-brands-invest-in-tried-and-tested-performance-channels/)

The mix of publisher types matters for this project. APMA reports that content publishers received 21% of affiliate investment in 2025. Price-comparison websites received 10%, subnetworks and aggregators 7%, and Comparison Shopping Services 5%. That is direct evidence that product discovery and comparison models occupy several meaningful parts of the channel rather than one narrow affiliate-site category.

It does not prove that a new garden or maker site will capture comparable economics. These figures describe established participants across many sectors and publisher sizes.

## Is affiliate still principally CPA?

Yes, but not exclusively.

APMA reports this 2025 UK investment mix:

| Payment mechanism                                       | Share of affiliate investment | Interpretation                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------: | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| CPA                                                     |                           81% | The channel remains predominantly performance-paid        |
| Tenancy fees                                            |                           13% | Fixed access and placement money is already material      |
| CPL                                                     |                            3% | Lead payments exist but represent a small measured share  |
| CPC assisted commissions and service or technology fees |                            2% | Several smaller models share the remaining measured spend |

The percentages do not sum to 100% because of rounding. Tenancy grew 18% year on year and increased from 11% to 13% of investment. CPA grew by 8%, broadly in line with the market. CPL fell from 4% to 3%. APMA also found that 24% of more than 100 surveyed brands allocated at least one fifth of affiliate spend outside last-click models; 36% of more than 100 surveyed UK publishers said more than one fifth of their revenue came from non-last-click payments. [APMA 2026 report](https://theapma.co.uk/download/9188/?tmstv=1777836706)

The correct conclusion is not that CPA is obsolete. It is that a serious publisher should understand CPA as the transaction spine and negotiate other payment types for value CPA cannot reliably measure.

## What do publishers say they want to be paid for?

impact.com's 2025 study surveyed 818 marketers, 284 publishers and 421 creators in eight countries including the UK. It is not a UK-only sample and respondents could select multiple payment types. Within the publisher sample:

| Model             | Currently received | Preferred | Direction of mismatch                                 |
| ----------------- | -----------------: | --------: | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| CPC               |                28% |       42% | Publishers want more payment for traffic they control |
| Fixed fee         |                30% |       36% | Publishers want more predictable income               |
| CPS               |                44% |       35% | Current usage exceeds preference                      |
| Hybrid            |                33% |       27% | Common but not universally preferred                  |
| Performance bonus |                27% |       27% | Broad alignment                                       |
| CPL               |                23% |       25% | Broad alignment                                       |
| CPM               |                15% |       19% | Limited but present appetite                          |

The same study says 47% of publisher programmes ranked income diversification as a leading goal and 44% reported growth in fixed-fee and paid-placement revenue. The study's interpretation is commercially important: publishers prefer payment for controllable outputs such as qualified traffic because brand-site conversion, price, availability and product quality sit outside the publisher's control. [impact.com State of Affiliate Marketing 2025](https://impact.com/research-reports/state-of-affiliate-marketing/), [full report](https://impact.com/downloads/research-reports/state-of-affiliate-marketing-report-1125.pdf)

The APMA and impact.com studies answer different questions with different samples. APMA measures UK investment flowing through networks and platforms. impact.com reports stated current and preferred compensation among international respondents. Their numbers should not be merged into one percentage. They point in the same direction: performance pay is central while publishers actively seek fixed or traffic-based protection against conversion risk.

## What does the publisher side say?

### Penske Media and StackCommerce: content is structurally under-credited by last click

Scott Ginsberg of Penske Media describes the central problem as introducing awareness, engagement and content into a last-click commercial system. A brand accustomed to high lower-funnel return expectations can see upper-funnel content as weak even when it initiated the eventual purchase.

StackCommerce and Penske responded by changing proposals and measuring what happened after content exposure. In a later Awin write-up, the partners describe a large electronics retailer whose directly attributed content return appeared close to 1:1. Assist analysis indicated that more than 20% of the value was being completed through other channels or partners. A retailer initially unwilling to put $5,000 into the content programme later became one of StackCommerce's largest spenders. This is one disclosed case rather than a general benchmark. [StackCommerce and Penske interview](https://www.awin.com/gb/awin-win-marketing-podcast/episode/stackcommerce-penske-media), [content-value analysis](https://www.awin.com/us/sector-insights/content-value-affiliate)

Implication: a specialist aggregator should capture comparison views, shortlist events, merchant clicks and later outcomes. It needs evidence for assisted influence before it asks advertisers to fund research or tenancy. Last-click revenue alone can undervalue the expensive editorial and product-normalisation work that creates the decision.

### Genie Shopping: approval and payment timing change the real economics

In the APMA payment panel, Warrick Lambert of Genie Shopping described pending transactions dating back to 2024 and said one advertiser's rejection rate moved from 40% to 80% after the publisher had already bought media. The APMA summary also records a practitioner estimate of around 100 days to payment outside travel. These are panel statements rather than audited industry averages.

The operational lesson is sound: headline commission is not publisher revenue. A publisher buying traffic needs approval-rate and time-to-cash cohorts by merchant. The same panel reports that faster-paying retailer relationships grew roughly twice as fast for Genie Shopping after it arranged accelerated payment. Again this is the speaker's case evidence rather than a universal causal result. [APMA payment panel summary and video](https://theapma.co.uk/apma-webinar-summary-fixing-affiliate-payments-to-unlock-growth/)

### The Independent: zero-click discovery weakens link-only attribution

The APMA's 2026 Erosion of the Link panel brings together Ed Hutchinson of The Independent, Aneira Henery of Love Holidays and Maura Smith of Partnerize. Its premise is that generative search and in-app discovery can use publisher knowledge without generating the click that conventional affiliate attribution requires.

The panel is valuable as a strategic warning rather than proof of a settled replacement model. Proposed ideas such as cost per influence remain emergent. The immediate response should be stronger first-party audiences, auditable content exposure, saved projects, email and direct merchant data—not booking forecast revenue against an unstandardised metric. [Erosion of the Link video](https://watch.getcontrast.io/register/theapma-erosion-of-the-link-affiliate-marketing-s-last-click-survival-guide)

## What does the advertiser side say?

### SharkNinja and Blue Light Card: placement can be bought with economics other than cash tenancy

Elliot Horn of SharkNinja and Holly Riley of Blue Light Card describe a relationship in which exclusive product offers receive prominent placement without a separate tenancy fee. The publisher values the exclusive consumer proposition and expects the increased conversion and commission to outperform a fixed placement package. SharkNinja values retaining cash that would otherwise pay for speculative tenancy and can deploy margin through a stronger discount instead.

This is not an argument against tenancy. It shows that placement has a negotiable consideration set:

- cash tenancy;
- higher CPA;
- an exclusive price or bundle;
- exclusive inventory or early access;
- a product sample and production support;
- guaranteed content rights;
- some combination of these.

The interview also demonstrates product-level commissioning and rapid merchandising around inventory priorities. That matters to a high-ticket aggregator: the advertiser may want a specific mower model, sauna package or kiln bundle moved rather than general brand traffic. [SharkNinja and Blue Light Card interview](https://www.awin.com/us/awin-win-marketing-podcast/episode/sharkninja-blue-light-card)

### Brand and agency buyers expect a package and an evidence trail

In an impact.com conversation about Q4 publisher buying, Todd Crawford and Lacie Thompson discuss flat-fee placement combined with revenue share, adjusted commission, newsletters, social amplification and content deliverables. They emphasise keeping historical records of each placement's cost, dates, commission uplift, traffic and customer outcomes so the next negotiation is based on evidence.

That supports a formal insertion-order product rather than improvised sponsorship emails. A merchant should know what is guaranteed, what remains editorial, what performance component applies and which audience/result data will be returned. [impact.com Q4 publisher-buying discussion](https://impact.com/podcasts/episodes/q4-is-closer-than-you-think/)

## What do agencies networks and tracking specialists say?

### Tracking failure is commercial leakage rather than a technical footnote

In Awin's conversation with Gen3 Marketing and Moonpull, Steve Brown describes affiliate as a CPA system that still needs a transaction connected back to the publisher click. The discussion covers broken first-party tracking, consent-banner interactions, tag changes and the degradation of tracking after an initially correct implementation.

The implication is that merchant tracking health belongs in offer ranking and commercial forecasting. If a merchant's feed is fresh but its tracking is unreliable, its nominal CPA is worth less. [Gen3 Marketing and Moonpull interview](https://www.awin.com/us/awin-win-marketing-podcast/episode/gen3-marketing-moonpull)

### Publishers need media kits even in a performance channel

In an impact.com publisher-growth discussion, Richa Dani argues that paid placements have become normal alongside CPA. The recommended publisher apparatus is practical: an updated media kit, clear promotional inventory, audience segmentation, seasonal opportunities and performance reporting. The interview distinguishes editorial review time from purchasable native placements and dedicated newsletters.

This supports establishing a commercial product catalogue before the site is large enough to sell it. The initial catalogue can list inventory as unavailable until audience thresholds are met. That is better than inventing a rate card after the first advertiser asks. [Fewer Delays More Paydays interview](https://impact.com/podcasts/episodes/fewer-delays-more-paydays-with-richa-dani/)

## What do established adjacent businesses prove?

### idealo proves direct merchant CPC exists for physical-product comparison

idealo's UK merchant pricing page says retailers are charged for clicks from compared offers to their shops. Its published UK standard price is £0.35 per click; Home & Garden and Leisure & Outdoors are also listed at £0.35. idealo states that merchant payment does not change organic offer ranking. [idealo merchant pricing](https://partner.idealo.com/partner-idealo-com/uk/pricing), [idealo consumer explanation](https://www.idealo.co.uk/magazine/faqs)

This proves the model exists. It does not prove a new publisher can charge the same amount or acquire equivalent merchant demand.

### MoneySuperMarket proves one aggregator can combine click fees sales commission and tenancy

MoneySuperMarket tells consumers that providers may pay:

- a flat amount following a purchase;
- a percentage commission;
- a fixed click-through fee;
- separate sponsored placement fees.

It states that sponsored results are labelled and do not alter the underlying comparison ranking. MONY Group's 2025 annual report goes further. Its provider products include clearly labelled Tenancy inventory, Market Boost data and insight products, and white-label B2B comparison services. Provider-service revenue grew 13% during 2025 according to the company. [How MoneySuperMarket works](https://www.moneysupermarket.com/how-moneysupermarket-works/), [MONY Group 2025 annual report](https://www.monygroup.com/application/files/3417/7304/3833/MONY-Group-plc-Annual-Report-2025.pdf)

The exact economics are not transferable from regulated financial comparison to garden products. The architecture is transferable: transaction revenue earns the initial audience; first-party decision data later supports advertising, insight and embedded B2B products.

### Future proves specialist publishing is a revenue portfolio

Future's 2025 annual report describes more than 150 specialist consumer brands monetised through advertising, affiliate ecommerce and magazines. Its B2C revenue profile includes digital advertising, affiliate ecommerce and subscriptions. Its B2B portfolio adds newsletters, webinars, lead generation, events and other media services. [Future 2025 annual report](https://futureplc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Annual-Report-and-Accounts-FY-2025-incl-Notice-of-AGM.pdf)

The useful lesson is structural rather than financial: specialist knowledge can be packaged into affiliate recommendations, advertising inventory, paid information and professional services. A new aggregator should not attempt all of those at launch, but it should avoid a product architecture that makes later diversification impossible.

### Autotrader proves dealer subscriptions can dominate a high-ticket marketplace

Autotrader's 2026 reporting separates retailer, private-listing, consumer-service and manufacturer/agency revenue. Average monthly revenue per retailer was £2,995 across 13,942 average retailer forecourts. Individual sellers also paid to advertise vehicles. The platform increasingly sells technology and data products alongside marketplace access. [Autotrader 2026 annual report](https://plc.autotrader.co.uk/investors/2026-annual-report/), [2026 full-year results](https://plc.autotrader.co.uk/media/etud5vca/full-year-press-release-fy26.pdf)

This is strong support for the dealer-subscription direction in smallholding machinery. It is not evidence that agricultural-equipment dealers will pay Autotrader-like fees. Network density and consumer demand must be built before seller subscriptions have comparable power.

## What directions does this open beyond a conventional affiliate site?

The evidence supports five different companies. They should not be blurred together in the operating plan.

| Direction                        | Customer paying                           | Primary unit bought                                      | Best candidate vertical                        |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Specialist decision publisher    | Retailer or brand                         | Validated sale plus selected media placements            | Garden; games rooms; maker studios             |
| Product comparison engine        | Merchant                                  | Qualified outbound click or completed sale               | Standardised garden products; tools; equipment |
| Project lead marketplace         | Installer or dealer                       | Accepted specification; booked survey; completed project | Awnings; garden rooms; cabins                  |
| Dealer inventory marketplace     | Dealer and private seller                 | Subscription; listing; qualified enquiry                 | Smallholding machinery                         |
| Data and decision infrastructure | Merchant; manufacturer; another publisher | Dashboard; dataset; API; embedded selector               | A later layer across any successful vertical   |

The first three can coexist inside premium garden systems because they solve adjacent parts of one customer project. The dealer marketplace is operationally different enough to remain a separate business thesis. The data product is an earned extension: it becomes defensible only after the platform has enough normalised offers, observed prices and buyer behaviour.

## What remains genuinely unknown?

This research still does not establish:

- whether the named garden merchants will approve this publisher;
- which will negotiate CPC, tenancy or hybrid terms rather than published CPA;
- merchant gross margin by product family;
- conversion and validation rates for the proposed traffic;
- how much a target installer will pay for an accepted specification;
- the minimum audience at which a target brand will buy tenancy;
- the real production cost of original tests and video;
- whether the target buyers use the decision tools as predicted;
- the cash reserve required for actual payment delays and reversals.

Those questions need direct interviews, merchant negotiations and controlled live traffic. The [primary interview programme](./14-primary-interview-programme.md) defines the next evidence gate without pretending those conversations have happened.
