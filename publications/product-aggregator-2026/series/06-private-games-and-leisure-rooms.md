# Private games and leisure rooms

## What is the business?

The business helps households and commercial buyers design, equip and maintain a complete games or leisure room. It is not a pool-table price comparison and not a generic gaming site.

Its promise is:

> Plan the room, choose equipment that fits, and buy it from a specialist who can deliver and support it.

The product range includes pool and pool-dining tables, pinball, arcade cabinets, jukeboxes, shuffleboard, table football, air hockey, golf simulators and selected home-cinema equipment. The common job is physical entertainment space, not video games as a media category.

## Who is the target buyer?

- Homeowners converting a spare room, garage, cabin or basement.
- Families buying one centrepiece product and later expanding the room.
- Collectors buying pinball, jukeboxes or restored arcade equipment.
- Hospitality venues, holiday lets and offices adding physical entertainment.
- Interior designers and installers specifying complete rooms.
- Buyers who need delivery, assembly, servicing and removal of existing equipment.

The buyer's anxiety is unusually concrete: Will it fit through the building? Is the room large enough to play? Can the floor carry it? Who assembles and services it? A useful aggregator answers those questions before showing the checkout link.

## What evidence supports the transaction model?

Home Leisure Direct reports:

- approximately £1,400 average order value;
- frequent sales around £4,000;
- 5% commission;
- a 30-day attribution window;
- approximately £70 commission on its average order;
- a range covering pool tables, jukeboxes, arcades, pinball, shuffleboard and other room equipment.

These are merchant-authored programme claims, observed on 3 September 2026. [Home Leisure Direct Awin profile](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/22809)

Liberty Games operates a second specialist programme covering games tables, arcade machines and related equipment, with installation and service capabilities described on its profile. [Liberty Games programme](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/7567)

The Games Room Company provides a further specialist retail and service substitute across pinball, pool, jukeboxes, foosball, shuffleboard and restored equipment. [Games Room Company](https://www.gamesroomcompany.com/)

The result is a credible retailer-checkout ecosystem, although merchant concentration remains material.

## How should the information architecture work?

### Plan a room

- Measure and model the room.
- Check cueing and player clearances.
- Plan power, lighting, sound and seating.
- Verify delivery paths, stairs and doorways.
- Build a phased budget.
- Save several room layouts.

### Choose a centrepiece

- Pool and snooker tables.
- Pool-dining tables.
- Pinball machines.
- Arcade cabinets.
- Golf simulators.
- Jukeboxes.

### Add supporting games

- Shuffleboard.
- Table football.
- Air hockey.
- Table tennis and convertible tables.
- Darts, scoring and wall protection.

### Buy, install and maintain

- Delivery and installation comparison.
- New, used and restored buying paths.
- Re-clothing, levelling, repair and relocation.
- Warranty and service coverage.
- Finance and total delivered price.

The room model should remain the persistent object. A user should be able to place a product, see the real operating clearance, replace it with another model and calculate what still fits.

## What comparison products should launch?

### Pool-table room planner

The planner should use table dimensions plus cue length and player movement, not just product footprint. It should model:

- room length and width;
- obstructions, radiators and doors;
- table dimensions and playing surface;
- cue lengths and compromised zones;
- dining conversion and chair clearance;
- floor level and access;
- delivery route;
- installation and future re-clothing.

The output should show layouts and trade-offs rather than pronounce one universal “best” table.

### New-versus-restored decision tool

Pinball, jukebox and arcade searches can include new, used and restored products. Comparison fields should cover:

- production year and manufacturer;
- restoration scope;
- originality and replacement parts;
- warranty;
- service history;
- delivery and setup;
- parts availability;
- seller specialism;
- expected maintenance.

A restored jukebox and a new reproduction are not variants of the same offer. Their identity and condition models must stay separate.

### Complete-room budget builder

The buyer should be able to choose a room archetype and see:

- equipment capital cost;
- delivery and assembly;
- floor, lighting and electrical preparation;
- acoustic work;
- seating and storage;
- service reserve;
- phased purchases.

The platform can then produce several retailer baskets while keeping installation-sensitive lines visible.

### Golf-simulator feasibility checker

Golf simulators carry higher CPC but also high order values and strict room constraints. The checker should model room width, depth, ceiling height, handedness, strike location, screen, projector throw, launch monitor, mat and enclosure. It belongs as a later premium extension after the simpler room planner works.

## Where is the competitive gap?

Specialist retailers already have large catalogues, guides, showrooms and technicians. Liberty Games publishes buyer guides across pool, pinball, arcade, shuffleboard and other categories. A new site's product descriptions will not beat that.

The gap is cross-merchant room planning and lifecycle truth:

- verify fit before purchase;
- compare total delivered and installed cost;
- distinguish new, used and restored condition;
- expose service and parts coverage;
- create multi-product layouts;
- recommend phased rooms at several budgets;
- retain a service history after purchase;
- support resale, relocation and upgrading.

The platform should become useful before and after the outbound affiliate click. That reduces dependence on winning one “best pool table” query.

## What are the economics?

Using Home Leisure Direct's reported figures:

```text
average order value = £1,400
commission rate = 5%
gross commission per average approved order = £70
vertical weighted CPC = £0.84
gross break-even visitor-to-approved-sale rate = 1.20%
```

If 60% of paid visitors click from the aggregator to a retailer, the implied retailer approved-sale rate is 2.00% before operating cost. At a 90% validation rate, it rises to approximately 2.22%.

Those calculations are scenarios around observed AOV, rate and CPC. They exclude merchant attribution loss, creator share, network timing, content cost, tax and fixed overhead.

More importantly, Home Leisure Direct's terms restrict PPC promotion. The attractive CPC cannot be treated as immediately commissionable paid acquisition through that programme. [Programme terms](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/22809)

The practical launch model should therefore combine organic room tools, direct traffic, email and merchants whose terms explicitly permit the intended acquisition path. Direct commercial agreements may be more valuable than assuming one network's default rules.

## How should creators participate?

Suitable creators include table technicians, arcade restorers, interior designers, golf-simulator installers and credible room owners. Their contribution should be a measured room or maintained collection, not a generic list of desirable products.

A creator room should disclose:

- real dimensions;
- product condition and source;
- delivery and installation experience;
- maintenance performed;
- total budget or documented bands;
- what they would change;
- which links are advertising;
- what they directly tested.

This gives the creator storefront an evidence standard and a reason to retain users beyond aesthetic inspiration.

## What would the launch wedge be?

Launch around pool tables and physical room planning:

1. Normalise 50–100 tables from at least two merchants.
2. Build a dimension and cue-clearance model.
3. Publish a browser-based room planner.
4. Add total delivered cost, installation and service fields.
5. Create ten measured room specifications for common UK spaces.
6. Add pinball, arcade and jukebox category guides without mass ingestion.
7. Instrument plans created, products placed, shortlists, retailer clicks and approved orders.
8. Obtain written PPC and content-policy confirmation before buying category traffic.

The room planner is the wedge because it creates proprietary user data and a reason for repeat sessions. Product comparison then becomes more than a list of prices.

## What would make this vertical fail?

- Merchant concentration gives one retailer effective control over the catalogue.
- Programme restrictions block the intended paid or editorial acquisition model.
- The site cannot obtain reliable dimensions, delivery and service data.
- Users treat it as inspiration but complete no measurable buying action.
- New, used and restored offers are collapsed into misleading comparisons.
- The room planner is visually impressive but does not change product choice.
- Retailers' own guides and showrooms remain more useful than the independent layer.
- High delivery and return complexity produces poor merchant validation.

## What remains unknown?

- Current commission and product-feed access for Liberty Games.
- Exact paid-search permissions across each merchant.
- Merchant conversion, validation, cancellation and return rates.
- The share of searches seeking used products.
- Whether room-planning users convert better than category-page visitors.
- Organic competition for product and room-planning queries.
- Commercial buyer share relative to households.

This is the best option if the goal is a fast, legible affiliate test. Its weakness is not demand or AOV; it is dependence on a small specialist retail ecosystem.
