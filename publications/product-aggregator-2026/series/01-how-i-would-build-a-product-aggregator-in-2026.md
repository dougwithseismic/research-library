# How I would build a product aggregator in 2026

I would build a product aggregator as a **decision company for one expensive customer project**, not as a database of products and prices. The public site would combine original editorial, project planning, calculators, compatibility rules, structured product comparisons, observed merchant offers and transparent affiliate attribution. Product aggregation would sit underneath a broader vertical such as premium outdoor living, serious maker studios or private games rooms.

I would start in the United Kingdom with premium garden and outdoor systems, but launch much more narrowly with a robotic-mower decision system for one property. A conservative basket across the eventual vertical produces 27,440 average monthly searches at a £0.94 volume-weighted historical CPC. It contains robot and ride-on mowers, awnings, greenhouses, pergolas, hot tubs, swim spas and outdoor saunas. Installed awnings, cabins and garden rooms add a separate quote-led lane. The vertical is wide enough to become a company; the first product should prove canonical identity, a versioned field-test method, compatibility, price history, saved buyer state and an installer handoff before that breadth is built.

The business would leave checkout with retailers and launch on CPA/CPS commission for tracked purchases, plus CPL or booked-survey fees where installation makes online checkout unsuitable. Once it can prove traffic and delivery, it would sell clearly labelled tenancy-plus-CPA packages, test merchant-funded CPC selectively and develop supplier data or software revenue. It would not aggregate traditional fashion, copy merchant descriptions, fabricate testing, or publish thousands of pages simply because a feed contains thousands of rows.

Shopping agents make that restriction structural. Merchant feeds can now pass directly into conversational product comparison, while generated descriptions, “best” lists and promotional imagery make generic product content abundant. The business therefore has to own original tests, compatibility logic, project constraints, verified offer history or installation outcomes that an agent can use but cannot truthfully invent. [Chapter 15](./15-shopping-agents-generated-content-and-the-decision-web.md) maps the bypass risk, responsible operating uses and revised kill conditions.

A forty-operator market pass now strengthens that conclusion. The durable operators own difficult systems: RTINGS has versioned test benches, PCPartPicker has project compatibility, BrickLink joins identity to bills of materials, idealo retains offer history, Chrono24 operates trust and Auto Trader enters supplier workflow. The weak pattern is a catalogue or article surface without a retained evidence or user loop. [Chapters 16–20](./16-the-product-aggregation-economy.md) map the economy, operators, mechanisms, failures and ownership evidence; [Chapter 21](./21-the-revised-founder-strategy.md) turns it into the narrower launch plan.

The supporting series contains the full [opportunity scorecard](./03-opportunity-landscape-and-scorecard.md), [unit-economics model](./08-business-model-and-unit-economics.md) and [catalogue architecture](./09-product-catalogue-and-data-architecture.md). ## Why is a product database no longer enough?

Merchant feeds already expose names, descriptions, prices, images, stock fields and deep links. Awin's own documentation says price-comparison partners are the primary users of its feed platform and describes direct access to millions of product details and prices. That makes feed access useful infrastructure, but it also means access itself is not scarce. [Awin product-feed guide](https://help.awin.com/developers/docs/product-feed-publisher-guide-intro)

Google's search policies identify copied product descriptions and cookie-cutter affiliate pages as thin affiliation. The same guidance gives the route out: original price information, testing, ratings, navigation and product comparisons that add meaningful value. [Google Search spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)

The implication is blunt. A feed-only aggregator has no durable reason to exist. It competes with Google Shopping, large comparison engines, retailers' own filters and any other publisher consuming the same file. It also inherits every feed error: duplicate products, stale stock, incorrect variants, truncated specifications and misleading sale prices.

The defensible layer begins where the feed ends:

- one canonical identity for the same product sold by several merchants;
- a category-specific specification model rather than free-text descriptions;
- compatibility and suitability rules;
- price and availability observations over time;
- original photographs, tests and installation notes where feasible;
- explicit reasons a product is suitable for one buyer and unsuitable for another;
- project calculators that translate a buyer's space, power, budget and use case into a shortlist;
- transparent retailer, commission and editorial policies.

That is a decision system. Product cards are merely one interface to it.

## What should the vertical represent?

The vertical should represent a job the buyer wants completed. “Robot lawn mowers” is a product category. “Make my garden easier to maintain and more useful throughout the year” is a durable customer project. “Pottery kilns” is a category. “Build a safe, capable creative studio at home” is a project.

A useful vertical has five boundaries:

1. **One recognisable buyer identity.** The same person can plausibly buy across several sections.
2. **One connected set of decisions.** Space, power, installation, maintenance, compatibility or workflow link the products.
3. **Several high-value transactions.** The opportunity survives if one category weakens or one merchant leaves.
4. **Enough editorial depth.** The brand can publish projects, tests, interviews, maintenance guidance and buying tools without manufacturing topics.
5. **A comprehensible promise.** A visitor can explain the site without listing every product it contains.

This is why robot mowers and made-to-measure garden awnings can share a website. Both belong to a premium outdoor-systems decision: automation, shade, structures and year-round use. Pottery wheels and kilns can share a different website with laser cutters, CNC routers, printmaking presses and workshop machines because the common job is designing a serious maker studio.

It is also why an indiscriminate “high-ticket products” website would fail. A golf simulator, compact tractor and pottery wheel may all be expensive, but they do not share a buyer, project, vocabulary or trust model.

## Which market would I choose first?

I would choose **premium garden and outdoor systems**.

These are not broad head terms. Every scored phrase expresses purchase or price intent. The total deliberately excludes `robot lawn mower`, `pottery wheel`, `hot tub` and other broad queries that may include informational interest. It also uses one canonical phrase per product family rather than adding every `buy`, `price` and `for sale` variation.

The merchant economics are credible. Navimow advertises a £1,000-plus average order value, 5%-plus commission and a 30-day cookie for qualifying robotic mower sales. [Navimow affiliate programme](https://uk.navimow.com/pages/affiliate-program) Pro-Line Direct reports average orders between £2,000 and £5,000 across saunas, hot tubs and wellness equipment. [Pro-Line Direct programme](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/122698) Steam & Oak offers a 5% standard commission across outdoor saunas, hot tubs and cold plunges. [Steam & Oak programme](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/124894)

The installed-project lane is different. `made to measure awnings` returns 140 average monthly searches at £2.27 CPC, while `awning quote` returns only 10 at £13.20. `garden room cost` returns 880 at £2.01. These buyers are valuable, but they require qualification, dimensions, postcode coverage, surveys and installer handoff. They should not be forced into the same checkout and attribution logic as a boxed product.

That creates a useful two-engine company:

- **Product engine:** comparisons and affiliate links for products that can be bought through a retailer.
- **Project engine:** calculators, specification capture and qualified introductions for installed or made-to-measure work.

## What would the website look like?

The home page would not be an infinite product grid. It would begin with projects:

- automate lawn care;
- create shade over a patio;
- choose a greenhouse for a specific garden;
- add a hot tub or outdoor sauna;
- build a garden office, cabin or studio;
- turn an underused garden into a year-round living space.

Each project would lead through the same sequence:

```text
problem → constraints → specification → shortlist → comparison → merchant or installer
```

A robot-mower buyer supplies lawn area, slope, boundaries, tree cover, narrow passages, theft risk and tolerance for boundary wire. The result is not “the ten best robot mowers.” It is a shortlist whose logic is visible.

An awning buyer supplies width, projection, wall type, orientation, controls, lighting, heating and postcode. The result is a budget band and a qualified route to suitable installers. Aura's current guide places most professionally installed, made-to-measure residential projects between £2,200 and £5,500, which is large enough to justify serious qualification. [Aura Awnings price guide](https://auraawnings.com/knowledge-hub/how-much-does-a-garden-awning-cost)

A greenhouse buyer supplies footprint, exposure, glazing preference, base requirements, growing ambitions and installation preference. The same answers can generate product recommendations, preparation content and accessory bundles without becoming a generic garden shop.

The `/compare/` area would contain the structured aggregator. The rest of the site would contain project guides, field tests, owner interviews, cost models, installation advice and maintenance. The aggregator is therefore a subsection of the brand, while the product graph powers most of the brand.

## How would I make the comparisons defensible?

Every category would have an explicit decision ontology. Generic fields such as brand, model, GTIN, price and stock are necessary but insufficient. The attributes that make a comparison useful are category-specific.

For robotic mowers:

- recommended lawn area and tested practical area;
- maximum claimed and tested slope;
- boundary-wire, RTK, vision or hybrid navigation;
- obstacle behaviour;
- passage width;
- cutting height and width;
- rain behaviour;
- theft protection;
- app and connectivity dependency;
- replaceable parts and warranty;
- installation burden.

For pottery kilns:

- chamber volume and usable dimensions;
- maximum temperature and firing profile;
- supply, amperage and plug type;
- controller;
- ventilation and clearance requirements;
- furniture included;
- lead time;
- service coverage and warranty;
- suitability for home, teaching or production use.

For pool tables:

- external dimensions and cueing clearance;
- slate or non-slate bed;
- room and floor requirements;
- dining conversion;
- delivery and assembly method;
- re-clothing and service availability;
- warranty and resale considerations.

The site should publish how every field is obtained: manufacturer specification, merchant feed, manual verification, original measurement or user report. Conflicting specifications should remain visible until resolved. Missing values should remain unknown rather than defaulting to a convenient answer.

## How would I obtain and maintain product data?

I would use affiliate feeds as ingestion sources, then normalise them into a platform-owned product graph. Awin feeds can include deep links, names, descriptions, prices, discounts, delivery, images and vertical-specific attributes. Its feed-list download includes last-update timestamps so a publisher can avoid downloading unchanged feeds. [Awin feed documentation](https://help.awin.com/developers/docs/product-feed-publisher-guide-intro)

The ingestion pipeline would have six stages:

1. **Capture raw feed versions.** Preserve source, time and checksum.
2. **Validate rows.** Reject malformed currency, URLs, identifiers and impossible prices.
3. **Resolve product identity.** Match GTIN, MPN, brand/model and controlled fingerprints.
4. **Map category attributes.** Convert merchant fields and extracted specifications into a typed ontology.
5. **Create offer observations.** Store price, stock, delivery and affiliate URL as time-bounded merchant observations.
6. **Publish only trusted entities.** Suppress stale, contradictory, duplicate or insufficiently described products.

Awin recommends at least daily feed updates because conversion falls when displayed prices disagree with the merchant. [Awin feed management](https://help.awin.com/developers/docs/en/managing-your-feed) I would therefore treat staleness as product behaviour, not a back-office warning. An old offer should disappear or carry a clear verification timestamp.

The canonical product must never be overwritten by the last merchant feed. A product and an offer are different objects. One product may have several merchants, prices, conditions, delivery promises and affiliate terms. The history of those observations is valuable both to buyers and to operations.

## How would the business make money?

The direct answer is not “CPC.” The Google Ads CPC figures in this research are acquisition-side observations: what the aggregator might pay for a search-ad visit. They are not evidence of what a merchant will pay the aggregator. A merchant-funded outbound CPC contract is a separate revenue instrument with different pricing, fraud, billing and traffic-quality obligations.

The first revenue layer for retailer-checkout products should be CPA or CPS: tracked affiliate commission on an approved sale. Checkout remains with the retailer, which means the aggregator does not initially hold stock, set delivery terms or become the merchant of record. This is not merely convention. The APMA's 2026 report attributes 81% of measured 2025 UK affiliate investment to CPA, compared with 13% to tenancy, 3% to CPL and 2% to a residual group containing CPC, assisted commissions and service or technology fees. [APMA State of the Affiliate Nation 2026](https://theapma.co.uk/download/9188/?tmstv=1777836706)

The second layer should be CPL or an outcome fee for installed projects. Installed awnings, garden rooms and some large machinery purchases need surveys, local service coverage or dealer contact. The platform can charge per accepted lead, booked survey, completed sale or through an exclusive territory agreement. The commercial event must be defined clearly: raw enquiry, qualified enquiry, booked survey and completed order are not interchangeable.

The third layer is creator-led distribution. A credible gardener, potter, fabricator or games-room installer can publish a tested collection or project specification. The platform attributes the downstream purchase and shares commission only after the merchant validates and funds it. This is stronger than generic influencer storefronts because the creator's authority is attached to a structured buying decision.

The fourth layer is clearly labelled tenancy: a fixed fee for defined access such as a launch package, comparison sponsorship or newsletter placement. Tenancy should not control rankings disguised as editorial judgement. Placement, commission and editorial score must be separate fields. The strongest later campaign product is likely to be a hybrid—fixed tenancy to fund guaranteed production and distribution, plus CPA or a performance bonus when the campaign sells.

Direct merchant CPC becomes credible only after the platform can prove valid, high-intent outbound traffic and operate click reconciliation. Dealer subscriptions and listing fees fit the smallholding marketplace. Merchant analytics, data licensing, white-label selectors, consumer membership and native checkout are later businesses, not launch assumptions.

The practitioner evidence and complete commercial decision are documented in [Industry evidence, public interviews and watchlist](./12-industry-evidence-public-interviews-and-watchlist.md) and [Revenue architecture](./13-revenue-architecture-cpa-cpc-cpl-tenancy-and-beyond.md). The short version is: **CPA is the spine; CPL handles projects; tenancy pays for guaranteed access; CPC is a selective comparison product; data and subscriptions are earned later.**

## Does a low CPC make paid acquisition viable?

No. Low CPC is a useful filter, not proof of profitable acquisition.

The simplest break-even relationship is:

```text
required approved-sale rate = CPC ÷ commission per approved order
```

For a £1,000 robotic mower at 5% commission, the maximum gross commission is £50. A £1.06 click requires approximately a 2.12% visitor-to-approved-sale rate before overhead. If only 60% of visitors click to a merchant, the merchant-side approved purchase rate must be roughly 3.53%.

For Home Leisure Direct's disclosed £1,400 average order and 5% commission, gross commission is £70. Against the leisure basket's £0.84 weighted CPC, break-even requires a 1.20% visitor-to-approved-sale rate before overhead. That arithmetic is attractive, but the merchant's programme terms restrict PPC activity; the business cannot assume those clicks are permitted or commissionable. [Home Leisure Direct programme](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/22809), [programme terms](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/22809)

The practical acquisition strategy is therefore blended:

- use paid search sparingly to validate landing pages and high-intent decision flows;
- build organic demand through original category expertise and tools;
- use newsletters, saved projects and price alerts to recover long consideration cycles;
- recruit specialist creators with real category credibility;
- negotiate direct merchant terms once conversion evidence exists.

## Why would I not begin with home wellness?

Home wellness has excellent transaction values. Pro-Line Direct reports £2,000–£5,000 average orders. The problem is acquisition efficiency and strategic overlap.

The standalone commercial basket—Pilates reformers, home and infrared saunas, massage chairs, ice baths and commercial exercise equipment—contains 2,830 average monthly searches at £1.63 weighted CPC. Infrared sauna, massage chair and ice-bath purchase terms sit around £1.61–£2.35. The basket is smaller and more expensive than garden, games rooms or maker studios.

Outdoor saunas, hot tubs, swim spas and cold plunges fit naturally inside premium garden systems. Folding them into that vertical produces merchant and content breadth without forcing a separate brand to compete for expensive wellness clicks.

## Why are games rooms and maker studios still compelling?

Private games and leisure rooms are the cleanest conventional affiliate business. The scored basket contains 9,670 commercial searches at £0.84 weighted CPC. Home Leisure Direct discloses a £1,400 average order value, 5% commission and a 30-day cookie; Liberty Games provides a second active specialist programme. [Home Leisure Direct](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/22809), [Liberty Games](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/7567)

The risk is concentration. A small number of specialist retailers control much of the relevant inventory, service and installation experience. The aggregator must add room planning, clearance calculations, restored-versus-new guidance and genuinely independent comparisons rather than reproducing retailer catalogues.

Serious maker studios have the best CPC profile: 4,940 commercial searches at £0.32 weighted CPC. Pottery wheels, kilns, lathes, milling machines, planer-thicknessers, etching presses and weaving looms all express technical buying decisions. Kiln Crafts lists a £1,199 entry wheel package and a £4,833 kiln-and-wheel studio bundle, and operates an affiliate programme aimed at art educators, pottery influencers and creative platforms. [Pottery wheels](https://www.kilncrafts.co.uk/collections/pottery-wheels), [studio bundle](https://www.kilncrafts.co.uk/products/nabertherm-top-45-kiln-and-gladstone-bailey-pottery-wheel), [affiliate programme](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/117821?setLocale=en_US)

Makers have lower demand but deeper trust requirements. A useful site must understand power, extraction, space, compatibility, workflow and safety. That creates more work and a stronger moat.

## Why is smallholding equipment a different company?

Compact tractors, mini diggers, wood chippers, log splitters, freeze dryers and trailers produce 11,850 high-ticket commercial searches at £0.59 weighted CPC. The demand looks exceptional.

The purchase path does not. `For sale` traffic in machinery mixes new equipment, used equipment, private sellers, dealers, auctions and sometimes hire. Product feeds and conventional affiliate checkout cover only part of the market. An existing specialist, Mini Diggers, monetises private and dealer listings, which illustrates the more natural model. [Mini Diggers listing plans](https://www.mini-diggers.co.uk/select-your-plan/)

I would pursue this only as a marketplace and lead product with dealer inventory, condition reports, finance, transport and inspection. It should not be inserted casually into an affiliate catalogue because its CPC happens to be low.

## What would I build in the first ninety days?

I would choose one vertical and one high-intent wedge, but design the schema for the broader project.

For premium garden systems:

1. Recruit or verify at least five monetisable merchants across three product families.
2. Obtain sample feeds and assess identifiers, specifications, stock, delivery, update frequency and permitted use.
3. Build a canonical catalogue for approximately 100 decision-relevant products, not 10,000 undifferentiated rows.
4. Launch one genuinely useful selector—robot mowers or greenhouses—with visible recommendation logic.
5. Publish ten authoritative project guides supported by original diagrams, interviews or measurements.
6. Implement tracked outbound links, sub-ID conventions and transaction reconciliation.
7. Test a small paid-search budget only where programme terms permit it.
8. Measure shortlist creation, merchant click-through, approved-sale rate, revenue per visit, feed freshness and return visits.

The first success criterion is not page count. It is proof that a buyer with a real constraint reaches a credible shortlist, clicks to a suitable merchant and produces a validated transaction.

## What would make me stop?

I would stop or change direction if any of these remained true after a bounded test:

- fewer than three serious merchants expose usable inventory in the launch categories;
- product identity cannot be normalised reliably enough to compare like with like;
- merchant terms prohibit the intended acquisition or presentation model;
- fewer than 20% of high-intent visitors use the comparison or shortlist function;
- fewer than 25% of engaged comparison users click to a merchant;
- approved revenue per high-intent visit remains below variable acquisition and operating cost after two landing-page iterations;
- original content and tools do not earn links, subscribers, direct traffic or returning users;
- the team cannot establish a review and price-verification process strong enough to publish honestly.

These are test thresholds, not observed benchmarks. Their purpose is to prevent a feed import and attractive search-volume table from becoming an indefinite project.

## What is the final recommendation?

Build **a premium garden and outdoor-systems decision brand**, not a robot-mower aggregator and not a general garden shop. Organise it around automation, shade and structures, growing spaces, outdoor wellness and garden buildings. Put comparison tools under `/compare/`, connect them to project guides and calculators, and use separate funnels for retailer checkout and installed quotes.

Use the existing affiliate-attribution architecture as the transaction spine: merchant programme, canonical catalogue, tracked outbound link, retailer purchase, transaction ingest, validation and commission ledger. Change the category and editorial premise, not the financial truth that checkout occurs elsewhere.

If the goal is the fastest pure affiliate test, launch private games and leisure rooms. If the goal is the most differentiated long-term publication, launch serious maker studios. Keep smallholding equipment as a separate dealer-marketplace thesis.

The company should be judged by how much uncertainty it removes from expensive purchases. If the product is only a sortable feed, it has not been built yet.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should the site compare every product in the vertical?

No. It should cover enough products to represent the serious choice set, then prioritise data quality and decision usefulness. A hundred well-normalised products with verified attributes are more valuable than ten thousand feed rows with copied descriptions.

### Should the aggregator show the cheapest price first?

Not automatically. The best offer may depend on delivery, installation, warranty, stock, finance, service coverage and merchant reliability. Price should be one ranked input, with paid placement kept separate from editorial order.

### Should checkout happen on the aggregator?

Not initially. Retailer checkout preserves the lower-risk publisher model and lets specialist merchants own delivery, returns and product support. Installed projects should use a qualified lead handoff. Native checkout is a later strategic decision with materially different consumer and operational obligations.

### Can paid search launch the business?

It can validate demand and landing pages, but it should not be assumed to fund the company. Programme PPC rules, click-to-merchant loss, merchant conversion, validation, returns and payment delay all sit between the initial click and usable commission.

### Is low CPC the same as low competition?

No. Nearly every retained commercial keyword has high paid competition. CPC is the historical price advertisers paid on average; it does not measure organic ranking difficulty or guarantee a future click price.
