# The twelve-month validation programme

## What happens next?

Run a buyer-first, provider-backed 50-site design programme. Do not start by building a national software platform or generating a large compliance-content library.

The programme has seven gates:

```text
framing → evidence → market → economics → AI disruption
→ adversarial review → validation
```

The desk-research gates are complete. Validation now crosses human seams: interviews, professional review, paid design partners, provider terms and an authorised report corpus. Those actions require real counterparties and have not been represented as completed.

## Weeks 1–2: recruit the evidence panel

Recruit:

- eight commercial-property managers across single-site and multi-site operations;
- eight responsible persons or facilities leads across office, retail, industrial, hospitality and care contexts;
- six fire-risk providers spanning solo, regional and multi-assessor firms;
- four asbestos providers with UKAS-accredited organisations represented;
- one qualified technical or regulatory reviewer per launched lane and jurisdiction.

Use structured interviews and retain permission, role, organisation type, portfolio shape and evidence limitations. Do not treat an anonymous web comment as a substitute.

### Buyer questions

- Which obligations create repeated administration, missed work or duplicate requests?
- Where are original reports and completion records stored?
- Who decides that a duty applies, approves the provider, funds remediation and accepts closure?
- Which incidents, audits, transactions or renewals expose evidence gaps?
- What does the current software and managing agent already solve?
- Would the buyer pay for evidence and coordination separately from the assessment?

### Provider questions

- What share of job time is site, travel, report, review, correction and customer follow-up?
- Which premises and briefs are rejected or repriced?
- Which credentials, insurance and access details must be known before booking?
- What information is repeatedly missing?
- Which software is already used and where does it fail?
- Can an authorised, redacted shadow study compare manual and AI-assisted work?

### Gate

At least five of eight property managers must describe the same expensive recurring workflow and a named budget owner must accept an evidence-and-coordination proposition. At least three fire providers must permit a measured shadow workflow. Otherwise narrow or stop.

## Weeks 3–6: professional review and manual service blueprint

Build an approved rule and escalation pack for one jurisdiction and a narrow premises class. Keep each statement attached to its source, geographical scope, effective date and applicability inputs.

The reviewer must explicitly inspect:

- dutyholder and responsibility wording;
- self-assessment and external-competence boundaries;
- review and change triggers;
- credential and insurance checks;
- report and action terminology;
- what the service may and may not advise;
- closure authority;
- customer and provider contract allocation;
- escalation to professional advice.

Map the manual workflow from first customer record to closure. Measure each handoff before automating it.

### Gate

A qualified reviewer gives scoped, versioned approval. Any material misstatement or unbounded responsibility stops publication of that operational rule and blocks product release until corrected.

## Months 2–3: sign paid design partners

Recruit three portfolio design partners covering at least 50 combined sites. The target fee range to test is £49–£99 per site per month, excluding third-party assessment and remediation. Discounting may be used for design access, but every partner must pay something so the test is not confused with polite interest.

Onboard through a manual evidence ledger:

1. resolve sites and jurisdiction;
2. identify roles and responsibility unknowns;
3. ingest original reports and registers;
4. map assets and lane applicability;
5. identify conflicts, missing evidence and open actions;
6. prepare provider briefs;
7. coordinate approved work;
8. retain completion packets and named acceptance.

Log operator time by activity and site. Record every correction, reopened action, duplicated request and unresolvable field.

### Gate

At least two partners remain paid and active for 90 days, use the action loop and provide authorised evidence. Stop if no paid conversion occurs or human service cost consumes contribution with no credible narrowing.

## Months 2–4: establish provider redundancy

For each live lane and region, qualify at least two providers. Verification covers legal entity, relevant people, scope, geography, credentials, insurance as appropriate, conflicts, capacity, quote basis, data delivery, corrections and escalation.

Agree operational terms for:

- accepted brief definition;
- access and cancellation;
- response and report timing;
- rejected work and credits;
- evidence format and customer ownership;
- responsibility and professional sign-off;
- remediation referrals and conflicts;
- incident and correction notification.

### Gate

Do not promise a lane or region with one provider. Stop that route if providers refuse usable completion evidence or the accepted-work contribution cannot carry coordination.

## Months 3–5: build the evaluation corpus

With explicit permission and redaction, assemble at least 100 historical reports, 500 critical fields, 300 site-note or photo packets where feasible, 200 provider-identity cases and 150 confirmed defect-to-action cases.

Create blinded gold labels and adjudicate reviewer disagreement. Include adverse examples:

- wrong or ambiguous jurisdiction;
- similar provider names;
- expired or limited-scope credentials;
- mismatched site and asset;
- draft and superseded reports;
- missing pages and poor scans;
- contradictory dates or actions;
- material-change events;
- invoices without completion evidence;
- malicious instructions inside uploaded documents.

### Gate

No AI capability enters live assistance without the relevant corpus and threshold. Public or synthetic records can test plumbing but cannot release safety-relevant behaviour.

## Months 4–6: shadow extraction and drafting

Run source-bound extraction, evidence conflict detection and report drafting beside normal human work. The output cannot update the customer record or reach a customer automatically.

Measure:

- wrong-jurisdiction rate;
- unsupported critical and non-critical fields;
- missing mandatory controls;
- unsupported observations and sentences;
- critical remedial-action errors;
- provider-match errors;
- reviewer time and material edits;
- false closure attempts;
- data leakage;
- operator cost per processed record.

Release thresholds are defined in the [evaluation plan](../evidence/ai-evaluation-plan.csv). Critical jurisdiction, field, observation, action, credential, closure, sign-off and leakage events have zero tolerance.

### Gate

Assisted drafting requires all critical thresholds to pass and median active reviewer time to improve at least 20% without worse safety metrics. Otherwise retain manual work or deterministic extraction.

## Months 6–8: release assisted records

Allow a compliance manager to accept proposed fields with side-by-side source evidence. Allow a competent assessor to accept narrative generated only from approved observations. Record material edits and rejection reasons.

Do not release autonomous messages or task changes yet. Use the period to detect automation bias: sample accepted output blindly, compare reviewer behaviour with manual cases and show unknowns before narrative.

### Gate

Accepted output remains stable across at least two monthly benchmark runs, no critical incident occurs and reviewer-time benefit persists. A safety regression returns the affected task to shadow mode.

## Months 7–9: bounded reminders and evidence requests

Release the first bounded agent on low-authority actions:

- create a reminder from an approved date and basis;
- request a named missing document;
- open an action from an accepted finding;
- prepare an appointment message for human approval;
- assemble, but not approve, a closure packet.

Permissions live outside the prompt. Messages and tasks include the accountable owner, source event and audit ID. Documents cannot instruct the agent. No agent can change or delete an accepted record, approve credentials, declare compliance or close work.

### Gate

Automation-attributable critical overdue actions remain below 2%, routing reversals remain below 5%, critical credential breaches remain zero and false closure remains zero. Any prohibited action disables the connector.

## Months 8–10: test lane expansion

Compare adding asbestos, legionella or lifting-equipment coordination. The next lane must have:

- repeated demand among current buyers;
- named technical authority;
- correct jurisdictional scope;
- two-provider regional redundancy;
- a distinct evaluation corpus;
- positive expected contribution;
- evidence that cross-lane records improve retention or reduce work.

Do not copy the fire template. Expand one lane at a time.

### Gate

At least three current customers request the lane and two accept a paid expansion. Otherwise remain narrow.

## Months 10–12: choose the company form

Use observed results to decide among four paths.

### Managed operator

Continue if recurring site contribution is positive, customers retain, provider coverage is redundant and AI improves work without critical failure.

### Vertical software

Separate a SaaS product only if repeated workflows are standard enough for customer or provider self-service, support cost is acceptable and users will pay independently.

### Acquisition

Begin confidential diligence only if owning delivery is clearly more valuable than partnering. Require three years of accounts, job-level contribution, claims, insurance, credentials, customer concentration, staff retention, report quality, open actions, software contracts and data rights.

### Remain out or partner with an incumbent

Choose this if the existing stack solves the problem, liability cannot be bounded, data does not accumulate or contribution depends on misleading duty claims.

## What is the operating dashboard?

### Customer value

- paid sites and 90-day retention;
- time from request to valid evidence;
- critical actions overdue;
- repeated requests avoided;
- provider response and completion time;
- reopened actions and recurrence;
- customer-reported audit, transaction or operating outcomes.

### Economics

- recurring revenue per site;
- provider coordination contribution;
- human minutes and cost per site;
- acquisition cost by channel;
- accepted-to-completed work rate;
- credits, cancellations and support;
- gross and contribution margin without capitalising assumed automation.

### AI quality

- unsupported critical fields, observations and sentences;
- wrong jurisdiction;
- credential match errors;
- critical anomaly recall;
- reviewer time and edit severity;
- false closure and unauthorised sign-off;
- leakage and security incidents;
- benchmark drift after every change.

### Defensibility

- percentage of records with field-level provenance;
- provider redundancy by lane and region;
- customer-authorised outcome links;
- direct and partner-originated demand versus search dependence;
- exports completed without data loss;
- decisions measurably improved by longitudinal evidence.

## What is the first reversible action?

The next action is to recruit the interview and professional-review panel using the exact sample and questions above, then obtain two paid portfolio design partners before building production automation.

No live interview, outreach, provider agreement, paid acquisition, site inspection or professional approval has been completed by this desk research. Those are the evidence-producing next steps, not details to be filled in later with assumptions.

## The twelve-month decision

At month 12, continue only if customers pay for recurring coordination, providers retain viable contribution, the service owns usable evidence with permission, and every released AI workflow meets its safety threshold. The target is not autonomous compliance. It is a defensible operation in which automation makes verified human responsibility easier to carry.
