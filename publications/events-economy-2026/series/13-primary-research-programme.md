# Primary research programme

## Why more evidence is required

Public sources establish that the category exists, how visible products are packaged and how incumbents describe their scale. They do not disclose the numbers that decide the business:

- first-to-second attendance;
- event contribution after labour and recovery;
- no-show and late-cancellation behaviour;
- active local demand by time and life stage;
- host recruitment and attrition;
- restaurant incrementality;
- serious incident rate and support load;
- membership utilisation and churn.

The next step is a bounded research and pilot programme, not another market-size estimate.

## Attendee interview guide

Ask about the last real attempt, not the ideal future.

1. When did you last try to meet someone new locally?
2. What did you do from deciding to go through arriving?
3. What nearly stopped you?
4. Did you attend alone? Why?
5. What did you pay, including food, travel and ticket?
6. What made the room feel comfortable or uncomfortable?
7. Did you exchange contact details or attend again?
8. What happened after the event?
9. What would make you refuse a hosted stranger dinner?
10. Which is worse: a small table, the wrong age mix, a noisy venue or a visible dating intention?
11. How far would you travel on a weekday evening?
12. Would you pay £12, £15 or £18 to reserve a hosted place before food?

Finish with a real commitment: an available date, deposit, waitlist signup or introduction. Do not count compliments as demand.

## Non-returner interview guide

1. What did the organiser promise?
2. What was different on the night?
3. How did arrival work?
4. Was anyone responsible for the room?
5. Did the group feel too small, too large or badly composed?
6. What did you do when uncomfortable?
7. Did the company contact you afterwards?
8. What would have earned a second booking?
9. Did price matter more before or after the event?
10. Did you tell anyone else about the experience?

Non-returners are more valuable than a page of five-star testimonials because they reveal the broken repeat loop.

## Venue interview guide

1. Which weekly slots have genuinely spare suitable capacity?
2. What table size works without harming normal service?
3. How long can the table remain?
4. What gross spend and menu structure are acceptable?
5. Who handles deposits, cancellations and split bills?
6. Which dietary and access needs can be handled reliably?
7. What has gone wrong with external group organisers?
8. Is a host meal contribution or comp possible?
9. What data would prove the group is incremental and worth repeating?
10. Would the venue pay after a measured pilot, and for what exact outcome?

Do not lead with sponsorship. Lead with operations and covers.

## Host interview guide

1. Describe the first twenty minutes of a difficult event.
2. What behaviour requires intervention?
3. What information is useful before arrival, and what is intrusive?
4. How many people can one host serve well?
5. How much preparation and follow-up is hidden?
6. What compensation makes the role sustainable?
7. What training would have prevented a past problem?
8. When should a host call the operator, venue or emergency services?
9. What should never be left to a host?
10. Would you host twice a month for six months?

## Field research

Attend or mystery-shop, subject to ordinary customer terms and no deception requiring false identity:

- two local hosted friendship dinners;
- two Timeleft formats in one UK city;
- two large singles events from different operators;
- one conventional speed-dating event;
- one Meetup or member-organised social;
- one non-commercial club or volunteer activity as a substitute.

Record the customer journey, not private attendee details:

```text
discovery
→ checkout disclosures
→ pre-event communication
→ arrival
→ realised group and venue
→ facilitation
→ safety visibility
→ end of event
→ follow-up and rebooking
```

Do not photograph, record or identify participants without explicit permission.

## Pilot design

Run six paid tables across two venues and two weekday slots.

| Variable   | Treatment                                                               |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cohort     | Broad adult versus life-stage guided                                    |
| Commitment | Refundable deposit versus £15 reservation                               |
| Hosting    | Trained host at every event; vary script intensity, not safety coverage |
| Venue      | Accessible quiet mid-price versus slightly higher-price setting         |
| Group size | Minimum 6, target 10, maximum 11 paid guests plus host                  |
| Follow-up  | Standard next-event offer versus mediated reconnect plus next event     |

Six events will not estimate a national market. They can expose whether arrival, attendance, hosting and payment work at all.

## Evidence capture

Maintain a versioned research ledger with:

- participant type and recruitment source;
- consent and permitted use;
- dated notes and direct quotes with identifiers removed;
- observed behaviour versus stated preference;
- hypothesis supported, challenged or unresolved;
- decision changed by the evidence;
- follow-up owner and date.

Interview records should be permissioned, access-controlled and de-identified. Published evidence should be aggregated and redacted.

## Decision meeting

At the end of the pilot, answer five questions with cohort evidence:

1. Can the same local cell fill repeatedly without founder subsidies?
2. Does a trained host create measurable comfort or repeat behaviour?
3. Is the £12–£18 reservation acceptable after the first experience?
4. Does direct event contribution remain after all delivery labour?
5. Is there one acquisition channel with two-event payback?

Choose one outcome:

- **continue:** repeat and contribution are credible;
- **narrow:** one cohort, slot or venue clearly works;
- **reprice:** service works but contribution does not;
- **change format:** demand exists but dinner or hosting does not fit;
- **stop:** interest does not convert into attended, repeated behaviour.

The purpose of primary research is not to validate the founder. It is to make stopping, narrowing or committing equally legitimate decisions.
