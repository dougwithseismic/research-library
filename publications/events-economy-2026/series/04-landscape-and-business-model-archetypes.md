# Landscape and business-model archetypes

## The market is a stack, not a category

A customer who wants a better evening can use a dating app, join a Meetup group, buy an Eventbrite ticket, attend a speed-dating night, subscribe to Timeleft, book a Dinners With Friends table, hear a Seed Talk, paint a pot, join a sports game, play D&D, browse Liiiv or ask a colleague to the pub. The relevant market is the set of alternatives for creating a credible occasion with other people.

The [52-operator atlas](./18-forty-two-company-operator-atlas.md) supplies the company-level evidence behind this landscape; the [structured-social-leisure chapter](./17-the-structured-social-leisure-economy.md) defines the market being compared.

The operators differ on three axes:

1. **Who creates supply?** The platform, a professional organiser, a local host, a venue or users themselves.
2. **What outcome is promised?** Friendship, romance, activity, professional community, entertainment or simply attendance.
3. **Who pays?** Attendee, organiser, venue, sponsor, employer or a mix.

## Dating apps: distribution with conflicted outcomes

Dating incumbents possess the largest consumer graphs, established payments and familiar safety tooling. Their economic engine is predominantly digital subscription and in-app revenue. A successful relationship can end a customer's use, while ongoing discovery can extend it. That does not mean the apps deliberately prevent success; it means the offline outcome and the revenue event are not perfectly aligned.

IRL events can improve that alignment. A venue ticket is monetised when the encounter occurs, even if no match follows. It also gives the platform a new kind of behavioural evidence: attendance, expressed interest and post-event connection.

## Dating-event brands: clarity and volatility

Thursday's live London listings show event-specific tickets—for example, a September 2026 over-30s matchmaking night advertised at £18 early bird and £25 general admission. The operator says it runs in 180 cities. [Thursday](https://www.getthursday.com/), [London event](https://www.thursday.com/events/london/thursday-30-matchmaking-clapham-copy-4)

The proposition is easy to understand and the gross revenue per large event can be attractive. The difficulty is composition. Romantic intent makes age, gender, orientation, perceived attractiveness and rejection more salient. Thursday's own safety material states that its operator does not conduct criminal background or identity checks on event attendees. [Thursday event safety](https://www.getthursday.com/issue-at-an-event)

An event-first dating business needs more than a promoter's ability to fill a room. It needs truthful capacity, a clear balance policy, trained staff, reporting, refund rules and a way to create value when no romantic match occurs.

## Algorithmic social calendars: membership plus operations

Timeleft removes profiles and planning from the front of the experience, matches small groups and coordinates venues. The membership pays for ongoing access rather than one guaranteed outcome. That can turn a lonely Tuesday into a repeat habit and smooth customer revenue.

The company, however, owns the empty-seat problem. Its terms explicitly say it does not guarantee attendance, gender ratio, age range or that participants have never met. It may charge a no-show penalty equal to 35% of the monthly subscription price. [Timeleft terms](https://timeleft.com/terms-conditions/)

Those clauses reveal the real operational variables: attendance, group size, novelty and member expectations.

## Hosted local friendship: service before software

Dinners With Friends uses a larger table than Timeleft's typical restaurant group and assigns a host who liaises with the venue, welcomes arrivals, starts introductions, supports conversation and helps with the bill. [Host a dinner](https://dinnerswithfriends.co.uk/host)

This substitutes human labour for product automation. It is valuable where digital density is low or where customers need a warmer arrival experience. It is also difficult to scale: the host is part facilitator, part customer support, part venue operations and part safety representative.

## Event marketplaces: picks and shovels

Eventbrite handled 4.6 million free and paid events, 258 million tickets and more than $3.0 billion in gross ticket sales—approximately £2.13 billion at the report's reference rate—in 2025 before its March 2026 acquisition by Bending Spoons. [Eventbrite 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1475115/000147511526000005/eb-20251231.htm)

Its UK fee page lists a 6.95% plus £0.59 service fee per paid ticket, with an optional 2% organiser fee for selected tools. [Eventbrite UK pricing](https://www.eventbrite.co.uk/help/en-ca/articles/755615/how-much-does-it-cost-for-organizers-to-use-eventbrite/)

The marketplace is enabling infrastructure and a distribution competitor. A new social operator should use existing ticketing until its own credits, membership, matching and settlement needs justify custom software. Rebuilding generic checkout does not improve the room.

## Community platforms: supply without a quality guarantee

Meetup charges organisers rather than primarily charging attendees. Its current Standard plan starts at $29.99 monthly (approximately £21.27) or $174.99 annually (approximately £124.11) for up to three groups; Pro starts at $55 (approximately £39.01) per group per month. Organisers can add dues or event fees. [Meetup organiser pricing](https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/28677808413197-Organizer-subscription-prices-overview)

Locals combines subscriptions and events with creator tooling. Its public pricing lists Pro at $69.99 per month (approximately £49.64) plus 3% transaction fees and Business at $249.99 (approximately £177.30) plus 1.5%. [Locals pricing](https://locals.org/pricing)

These models scale supply creation by pushing curation, conduct and event quality to organisers. A managed social-events company does the opposite: it owns more quality and therefore more cost.

## The unoccupied position

The clearest open position is not a new generic platform. It is a **regional event house that connects public interest to private relationship depth**:

- a distinctive activity or editorial programme rather than friendship as the only purchase reason;
- solo-friendly arrival and hosting;
- larger public rooms that can acquire economically;
- related small cohorts that create repeat faces;
- adult life-stage sensitivity without turning the whole brand into an age label;
- transparent format-level economics;
- a narrow service radius and published expansion gates;
- no requirement to build a public profile or perform online popularity.

That position can eventually add subscriptions, partner inventory, media, employer distribution and software. It should begin by earning the right to fill the same programme twice and move a customer from the large room to the small one.
