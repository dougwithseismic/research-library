# Portfolio capital, concentration and value destruction

## Portfolio arithmetic is not a forecast

The [capital scenarios](../evidence/portfolio-capital-scenarios.csv) model five states using explicit assumptions. They are not valuations or expected results. Their purpose is to make operating scope visible.

| Scenario                           | Active cells | Monthly gross revenue | Contribution before founder pay | Indicative capital need |
| ---------------------------------- | -----------: | --------------------: | ------------------------------: | ----------------------: |
| Three-month validation lane        |            1 |                  £893 |                           -£358 |                 £3k–£7k |
| One mature cell                    |            1 |                £4,186 |                          £1,936 |                £8k–£15k |
| Four-cell bootstrap portfolio      |            4 |               £18,840 |                          £9,340 |               £25k–£60k |

The national scenario has much more revenue and only modest modelled contribution because support, trust, sales, technology and acquisition rise with liquidity ambition. This is consistent with the filing lesson: marketplace scale is not passive margin.

## Scenario 1: buy evidence

The first £3,000–£7,000 funds buyer discovery, call or form attribution, a small paid-intent test where justified, original content, qualification labour and legal/privacy review. The model expects a small loss.

The return is a decision:

- one buyer retains and the cell advances;
- demand exists but the model changes;
- the buyer cannot convert and is replaced;
- the cell fails before a full SEO build.

Avoiding six months of content in a non-buying market is a successful outcome.

## Scenario 2: one cash-flow cell

The illustration assumes 45 qualified events a month, 78% acceptance, £105 realised revenue per accepted event and £500 fixed monthly revenue. It produces £4,186 gross revenue and £1,936 contribution before founder pay.

That is a useful small asset but fragile:

- one algorithmic surface;
- one city-service demand pool;
- one or two buyers;
- one routing failure;
- one regulatory or platform interpretation;
- founder dependency.

It should finance redundancy, not lifestyle extraction, until those concentrations fall.

## Scenario 3: bootstrap portfolio

Four mature cells might produce meaningful founder cash flow. They should be sequenced so that the first cell funds and teaches the second. A common qualification ledger and buyer reporting system can create operating leverage; four unrelated websites with four definitions do not.

Capital allocation gates:

1. **Research allocation:** buyer pain and service economics evidenced.
2. **Validation allocation:** one paid or partner-distributed cohort produces accepted events.
3. **Organic allocation:** content and authority receive capital only after buyer retention.
4. **Replication allocation:** adjacent market cell opens only after process stability.
5. **Infrastructure allocation:** custom software follows recurring manual bottlenecks.

## Scenario 4: regional routing company

At twelve cells and roughly £1.16 million annualised model revenue, the company needs dedicated buyer success, qualification QA, finance and billing, privacy operations and data engineering. Buyer concentration, service uptime and management control determine value more than domain count.

Potential acquirers would diligence:

- retained gross revenue by buyer cohort;
- traffic-source concentration;
- branded and direct demand;
- accepted-event gross margin;
- buyer and provider concentration;
- outcome-data completeness;
- compliance incidents and unresolved disputes;
- dependency on founder relationships;
- content and link provenance;
- ownership of numbers, domains, code and data rights.

## Scenario 5: venture marketplace

The Airtasker UK filing describes millions of pounds of media inventory and parent financing through convertible notes. That does not make venture funding wrong. It shows what national marketplace ambition can consume.

Venture capital becomes plausible only if the thesis requires rapid multi-sided liquidity, brand investment and a transaction or workflow network with large expansion value. A four-site local lead portfolio does not need venture capital and would be harmed by venture return expectations.

## How local lead portfolios destroy value

### They build before a buyer exists

Search demand is researched in detail while the buyer is represented by an imagined “local contractor.” Months of sunk cost then meet silence or a low willingness to pay.

**Control:** obtain buyer interviews, a written pilot definition and preferably a paid commitment before authority investment.

### They treat CPC as price

A £20 click becomes a claim that a lead is worth £100. The operator ignores click-to-lead rate and the fact that only the fulfiller owns job contribution.

**Control:** model from buyer gross contribution backward and show funnel sensitivity.

### They use false local identity

Virtual offices, borrowed addresses, fake teams and profiles create suspension, consumer-deception and brand risk. Google's eligibility guidance explicitly excludes lead-generation companies from Business Profiles.

**Control:** operate transparently; partner profiles belong to real eligible providers.

### They create doorway portfolios

Dozens of city pages repeat the same claims with changed place names. They add no local evidence and fit Google's doorway and scaled-content risk patterns.

**Control:** open a market cell only when it has distinct buyers, service facts and original data.

### They depend on one buyer

A tenant's non-payment becomes zero revenue. The operator cannot replace them without requalifying the service promise.

**Control:** maintain vetted fallback capacity without secretly selling exclusivity twice.

### They depend on one source

One ranking supplies nearly every event. An update, Local Services Ads expansion or AI interface change erases the funnel.

**Control:** build direct brand, referrals, partner distribution and a first-party data utility.

### They ignore call operations

Leads ring unanswered, recordings are unavailable, calls route outside hours and disputes are decided by memory.

**Control:** monitor delivery, pickup, duration, reason codes and escalation from day one.

### They oversell verification

A directory check becomes a guarantee, an expired insurance certificate remains live, or copied reviews appear first-party.

**Control:** record source, scope, checked date and expiry; explain what verification does not establish.

### They scale negative contribution

Revenue rises while qualification labour, ad cost, credits and support rise faster. Gross lead margin is mistaken for company margin.

**Control:** calculate contribution per accepted event and per cell after direct operations.

### They cannot prove outcomes

Traffic and call counts exist, but no quote or win data. The asset cannot improve, price or survive buyer churn intelligently.

**Control:** make outcome reporting part of the buyer product and contract.

## Kill, hold and invest rules

**Kill a cell** when two capable buyers reject the economics after measured pilots, accepted-event contribution remains negative after two controlled iterations, or compliance requires capabilities outside strategy.

**Hold a cell** when demand is evident but seasonality, buyer capacity or data quality prevents a decision. Keep the domain and research; stop authority spend.

**Invest in a cell** when at least one buyer has paid and retained, acceptance and outcome data are stable, direct contribution is positive, and the next spend has a testable mechanism.

## The bootstrap recommendation

Use founder capital for one evidence lane. Keep fixed costs low and preserve the option to stop. Reinvest only realised contribution. Consider external capital after repeatability across at least three cells and only when the capital buys distribution, data or workflow that compounds across the network.
