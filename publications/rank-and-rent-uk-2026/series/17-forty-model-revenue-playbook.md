# The forty-model revenue playbook

## There is no single rent

The expanded [revenue playbook](../evidence/revenue-playbook.csv) contains 40 architectures. It is an option set, not a recommendation to launch 40 products. The correct model depends on when value becomes observable, who controls fulfilment, how much attribution the buyer will share and which party carries quality risk.

The models fall into seven families.

## 1. Pay for a qualified event

The simplest family charges for a measurable step:

- qualified connected call;
- exclusive accepted lead;
- shared accepted lead;
- attended appointment;
- completed quote;
- confirmed won job.

Each step moves closer to revenue and transfers more risk to the publisher. A call is fast to verify but may not be a serviceable job. An attended appointment has more value but introduces cancellation and reminder operations. A won-job fee creates alignment but needs a long attribution window and buyer honesty.

The billable event must be a contract definition, not a sales adjective. “Qualified” should specify geography, service, decision-maker status, contactability, duplicate period, minimum call duration, operating hours and exclusions. Credit rules require evidence and a deadline.

**Best first models:** qualified call for Bristol drainage; exclusive accepted lead for Manchester compliance work; attended appointment for Edinburgh boilers.

## 2. Share the downstream outcome

Revenue share, gross-profit share and won-job bonuses can capture more value than a lead fee. They are attractive where the operator has one deep partner and can audit outcomes.

They also fail in predictable ways:

- the buyer does not tag the source;
- a sale closes outside the agreed window;
- gross revenue is confused with collected revenue;
- refunds, finance failures and cancellations are not reconciled;
- the buyer understates wins;
- the publisher expects indefinite commission from a one-off introduction.

A revenue-share contract should define source, customer identity, attribution window, tax, refunds, payment date, evidence rights, recurring revenue, existing customers and termination. A simple accepted-lead price is superior when the data relationship cannot support this.

## 3. Sell access or scarcity

Fixed territory rent, category subscriptions, minimum commitments, prepaid credit packs and tiered memberships sell controlled access rather than one outcome.

The attraction is predictable cash. The danger is that a monthly price hides deterioration until the buyer cancels. A territory should not be rented before the operator has at least 90 days of accepted-event volume and buyer outcome data. Even then, the agreement should include a service-level range rather than an impossible lead guarantee.

Checkatrade, Rated People and TrustATrader demonstrate variants of this architecture: free or low-friction entry, paid tiers, area or category controls, and usage or opportunity access. The independent operator can use the same logic at smaller scale:

1. a low base covering attribution and reporting;
2. an included event allowance;
3. an agreed overage price;
4. a monthly cap linked to buyer capacity;
5. service credits only for defined failures.

This is more resilient than one opaque “site rent” number.

## 4. Monetise trust and visibility

Sponsored profiles, verified profile subscriptions, reputation software and assurance or protection fees monetise consumer confidence.

Sponsored placement must be recognisable as advertising and cannot override suitability. Verification has ongoing cost: Companies House status, insurance, scheme membership and professional registers can change. Reviews require provenance, moderation and challenge processes.

The Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act regime has applied to relevant unfair practices since 6 April 2025. CMA guidance addresses fake reviews and hidden material information, and 2026 enforcement activity shows this is not theoretical. [CMA unfair commercial practices guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207/unfair-commercial-practices) [CMA fake-review investigations](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/fake-and-misleading-reviews-5-businesses-under-cma-investigation)

Do not sell a badge that implies approval the operator has not genuinely established.

## 5. Become workflow software

Call-tracking resale, lead-routing software, quote and invoice tools, review management, conversion APIs and data benchmarks move the product into daily operations.

Workflow can create higher retention than traffic because it stores routing logic and performance history. It also creates a software company:

- reliable delivery and retry logic;
- role-based access;
- audit logs;
- data retention and deletion;
- number provisioning and KYC;
- API schemas and versioning;
- support when a live opportunity fails;
- integrations with buyer CRM and calendars.

The first cell should be designed with these concepts but should not build a full SaaS product. Manual operations are acceptable while events are few, provided the audit trail is sound.

## 6. Sell adjacent information or services

Procurement packs, tender alerts, marketing retainers, white-label wholesale feeds and managed exclusive-area acquisition expand beyond consumer forms.

Tradello's public offer is a useful example: £24.99 for a trade-specific pack derived from public procurement records, with the route to the buyer, and no claim that the contractor will win. [Tradello](https://www.tradello.co.uk/) The product monetises research, timing and normalisation rather than personal data.

Commercial fire, cleaning and facilities markets may suit opportunity intelligence better than call resale. The buyer values the contract size, incumbent timing and decision-maker route. Sales cycles are longer, so recurring alerts or research subscriptions can make more sense than per-form billing.

An agency retainer is another valid outcome. If interviews reveal that contractors want their own asset rather than rented demand, the research can support a vertical SEO and conversion service. That is not failure; it is a different business with labour and client concentration.

## 7. Own or finance fulfilment

Transaction take rate, booking fees, warranties, owned service margin, minority revenue partnerships, franchise fees and acquisitions capture the largest share of customer value.

They require the largest scope:

- payments and refund operations;
- consumer support and dispute resolution;
- licences, registrations and insurance;
- worker or subcontractor controls;
- quality, warranty and claims;
- cash tied up between acquisition and collection;
- operational leadership beyond search marketing.

Owned fulfilment becomes rational only when the demand data repeatedly exposes a strong cell and acquisition is constrained by buyer capability. It should be entered deliberately, not because a tenant stopped paying.

## A model-selection decision tree

Start with the latest reliably observable event.

1. **Only a call can be verified:** sell a qualified call.
2. **The form can be validated and accepted:** sell an exclusive accepted lead.
3. **The calendar is integrated and attendance known:** sell an attended appointment.
4. **The buyer reliably reports quotes and wins:** use base-plus-performance.
5. **The portfolio has stable event volume but variable buyer capacity:** add minimums, caps or territory access.
6. **Buyers use the routing and reporting every day:** charge for workflow.
7. **The operator controls fulfilment and payments:** consider a take rate or owned margin.

Do not choose a downstream model merely because its headline price is higher. Choose the deepest event that can be independently reconciled without disproportionate overhead.

## Model combinations worth testing

### Urgent-service cell

- prepaid qualified-call credits;
- daily volume cap;
- replacement for non-serviceable events;
- optional £60–£110 monthly attribution/reporting fee;
- no fixed territory promise until a 90-day cohort exists.

### Compliance-specialist cell

- exclusive accepted lead;
- structured property brief;
- base fee covering verification and reporting;
- success fee for a disclosed portfolio or high-value instruction;
- no professional recommendation by the publisher.

### High-ticket installation cell

- confirmed attended appointment;
- replacement policy for no-access or ineligible property;
- lower appointment charge plus verified win bonus;
- automated reminders;
- buyer capacity calendar.

### Mature regional network

- minimum monthly commitment;
- API or CRM delivery;
- tiered accepted-event overage;
- branch-level caps;
- quarterly cohort pricing review;
- optional white-label reporting.

## What to reject

Reject shared leads without explicit sharing disclosure. Reject fixed rent based only on a ranking screenshot. Reject lifetime revenue share without outcome access. Reject guarantees the operator cannot control. Reject consumer booking fees revealed late. Reject financial or regulated-service referrals until the legal permissions are clear.

The best initial contract is intentionally boring: one buyer, one cell, one definition, one cap, one credit policy, one monthly report and a termination route.
