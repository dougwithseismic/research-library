# The complete UK rank-and-rent report

## Executive decision

There is a real UK business here, but the familiar “digital landlord” story is the wrong operating model for 2026.

I would build a **transparent local demand and qualification company**, initially in one market cell. It would own the organic website, the data model, the call and form routing, the lead-quality record and the commercial relationship. It would not create fake local contractors, virtual offices or speculative Google Business Profiles. A genuine service business would own and manage any eligible profile associated with that business.

The deeper validation order is:

1. **Fire risk assessment in Manchester** — the final expanded explicit-city basket has 550 searches at £26.02 weighted CPC; the matching local-geo basket has 280 at £9.10. It has B2B and compliance intent, but the publisher cannot imply that it performs safety-critical work or that every responsible person must buy a third-party assessment.
2. **Asbestos surveys in Manchester** — the explicit basket has 290 searches at £19.50 weighted CPC and the local-geo basket 290 at £13.31. The methods agree strongly, but fulfilment must use technically competent, preferably accredited surveyors and keep survey advice separate from removal incentives.
3. **Drain unblocking in Bristol** — 1,600 explicit-basket searches at £14.85 weighted CPC and 690 local-geo searches at £6.66. It is the fastest call-funnel test, but only works with live answer coverage, truthful service areas and rapid dispatch.
4. **Boiler installation in Edinburgh** — 1,920 explicit-basket searches at £19.39 weighted CPC and 420 local-geo searches at £13.67. The order value can sustain meaningful acquisition economics, but a verified Gas Safe partner, reviews and a genuine local presence are non-negotiable.

These are markets for buyer interviews and controlled acquisition tests, not four simultaneous site builds. Bristol loft conversions, Nottingham garage-door repair and a national or regional spray-foam-removal publication form the second line. Spray-foam removal is not a conventional city site: the UK generic phrase returned 1,600 searches at £26.06 CPC, while all 26 city-modified phrases together returned only 380. That pattern calls for a specialist regional demand site, not 26 cloned location pages.

**Commercial cleaning in Edinburgh is downgraded from first choice to pause.** The 2,900-search headline phrase is dominated by an April 2026 spike and the expanded explicit basket disagrees sharply with a 90-search local-geo basket that reports no average CPC. The local supplier market is real; the search-demand thesis needs independent verification.

I would not start in London despite its large demand. Its 35-phrase sample produced 40,970 searches and a £16.77 weighted CPC, but that same auction value attracts national brands, directories, well-reviewed local operators and aggressive search advertising. London becomes attractive after the funnel and buyer model are proven elsewhere, or where a partner already has operational coverage and reputation.

I would also avoid beginning with mortgages, personal injury, dental implants, private ADHD assessment or funeral services. Several show attractive volume and CPC, but the financial, legal, health, advertising, privacy and vulnerability risks make them poor learning environments for a new operator.

The main conclusion is therefore conditional: **build it if buyer demand, fulfillment quality and accepted-lead economics are validated before heavy SEO spend; do not build a speculative network and hope tenants appear later.** AI makes this more stringent. Generated service and location copy is abundant, answer interfaces compress generic research, and platforms can increasingly qualify the enquiry. The proposed asset must be verified supply, request structure, buyer rules and outcome data—not generated pages or a chatbot wrapper.

## What “rank and rent” means after the old playbook is removed

The traditional model has four steps: register an exact or partial-match local domain, publish pages for a trade and city, rank the site and a Google Business Profile, then rent the calls or entire asset to a contractor. The commercial attraction is obvious. The publisher funds the search risk, controls the asset and sells an already-performing acquisition channel rather than an SEO promise.

Three things make the old version fragile.

First, Google explicitly lists **lead generation agents or companies** and online-only businesses as ineligible for a Business Profile. Only owners or authorised representatives may verify and manage a profile. Google also says a profile's phone and website should be the business's single authoritative contacts and that the website content must be owned and managed by the business owner. [Google Business Profile eligibility and ownership](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en)

Second, Google's web-spam policies define doorway abuse to include multiple regional or city pages that funnel users to one destination and define scaled-content abuse as large amounts of unoriginal content made primarily to manipulate rankings. Buying links for ranking credit is also explicitly listed as link spam. [Google Search spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)

Third, AI Overviews and AI Mode absorb more of the research journey. The resulting site cannot depend on generic articles such as “how much does a plumber cost?” while adding no first-party value. It must win either the direct action query or provide information that Google and users cannot obtain from a generic synthesis: real availability, verified credentials, service-specific price structure, local response evidence, original photos, qualification logic and an accountable handoff.

That leaves four defensible forms:

- **Publisher-owned organic lead utility.** The brand is openly an intermediary and routes qualified enquiries to disclosed providers.
- **Tenant-led local demand site.** A real provider owns or manages the site and its eligible profile; the publisher supplies the platform and acquisition operation. This sacrifices some “landlord” control but aligns with Google's profile rules.
- **Marketplace or affiliate layer.** The site routes users into inventory or project marketplaces and earns a tracked payout. It has less control but is useful before an exclusive buyer exists.
- **Owned operator or joint venture.** The publisher participates in fulfillment and economics. This has the best alignment and most liability.

The business asset is no longer merely a domain that ranks. It is the **demand history, accepted-lead data, qualification workflow, buyer bench, local brand evidence and reproducible acquisition system**.

## The demand screen

The project submitted 910 explicit `service + city` phrases: 35 niches across 26 UK cities. It separately submitted each generic service phrase and each `near me` form, adding 70 national-intent observations. All used Google Search, English language criterion 1000 and United Kingdom geo criterion 2826 through Google Ads API v25 on 3 September 2026. The working account currency is GBP.

The most important methodological constraint is that `drain unblocking Bristol` measured under a UK target is not the same observation as `drain unblocking` measured inside a Bristol geo target. The deeper pass therefore added 144 generic-service observations inside nine city geo targets and expanded explicit and local-geo variants for seven priority clusters.

An earlier `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED` response was a transient service-rate limit, not evidence that the account had run out of keyword searches. Google documents historical-metrics requests at one per second per customer ID on a rolling window. Calls succeeded after adding 1.5-second pacing and a bounded delayed retry. This is not a geolocated Google pack audit. It does show the minimum competitive object. A successful page needs more than a rewritten service description. ## The opportunity portfolio

### First wave: validate now

**Asbestos surveys × Manchester.** The explicit and local-geo CPC evidence agrees better than any other priority cluster. HSE says surveyors should be technically competent and strongly recommends accredited surveying organisations; UKAS is the recognised accreditation body in Great Britain for asbestos inspection bodies. Build provider verification and report turnaround into the product. Checkatrade's 2026 guide places a domestic management survey around £250 and a commercial survey around £400, which means apparent CPC value does not automatically leave room for a large lead fee. [HSE asbestos survey guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/asbestos/duty/arrange-asbestos-survey.htm) [Checkatrade asbestos survey costs](https://www.checkatrade.com/blog/cost-guides/asbestos-survey-cost/)

**Fire risk assessment × Manchester.** Build the qualification layer around premises type, use, floors, sleeping risk, occupancy, floor area, previous assessment, significant changes and desired timing. GOV.UK says the responsible person must conduct and regularly review an assessment; a competent person is needed where the responsible person lacks expertise or time. It is not always mandatory to buy a third-party assessment, so pages must not claim otherwise. [GOV.UK fire risk assessments](https://www.gov.uk/workplace-fire-safety-your-responsibilities/fire-risk-assessments)

**Drain unblocking × Bristol.** Use phone-first acquisition with form fallback. Capture caller postcode, private versus shared drain, affected fixtures, sewage backup, internal or external location, access, urgency and permission to attend. Checkatrade's 2026 ballparks show an average £60 call-out, £180 emergency call-out, £85 rodding and £185 jetting; those values explain why a £16 click can still be expensive relative to a simple job. [Checkatrade drain cost guide](https://www.checkatrade.com/blog/cost-guides/drain-unblocking-cost/)

**Boiler installation × Edinburgh.** Use a quote path for replacement rather than repair. Capture fuel, current system, property type, bedrooms, bathrooms, boiler position, flue, timing and finance interest without making unauthorised financial promotions. Checkatrade estimates an average gas-combi supply-and-install job around £3,000. Any gas work must be performed by a suitably qualified Gas Safe registered business or engineer. [Checkatrade boiler costs](https://www.checkatrade.com/blog/cost-guides/new-boiler-cost/) [HSE Gas Safe guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/gas/domestic/newschemecontract.htm)

### Second wave: partner-led tests

**Garage-door repair × Nottingham.** This is a smaller, operationally simpler call market. The result set includes established repair firms with visible diagnostic and repair pricing, so a new publisher needs better triage or availability rather than thin content.

**House clearance × Newcastle.** Capture postcode, photos, property size, access, approximate volume, high-cost items, reusable goods and desired date. GOV.UK tells consumers to check a private skip or house-clearance business on the relevant waste-carrier register. The lead utility can make that verification visible. [GOV.UK household waste](https://www.gov.uk/dispose-household-waste)

**Loft conversion × Bristol.** High CPC and high project value support appointment economics, but the cycle is long. The site should qualify property type, roof structure, conservation status, access, intended room, budget and desired design stage. Attribution should cover months, not a seven-day last-click window.

**Spray-foam removal × regional UK.** Build a fact-heavy specialist guide and inspection funnel, then monitor demand durability. The generic/city mismatch argues against mass city pages. This market also demands restrained claims because homeowners may be responding to mortgage or survey problems.

### Paused pending query verification

**Commercial cleaning × Edinburgh.** The commercial product would still be a premises-qualified site visit with sector, area, frequency, contract timing, access and specialist tasks. Companies House filings identify a spectrum of plausible suppliers. But the search series is too anomalous to justify a build: the expanded explicit basket's April peak is 7.87 times its monthly mean, while the local-geo basket reports only 90 average searches and no average CPC. Retest the queries or obtain real impression data first.

### Markets that need a different model

Self storage and skip hire are strong search markets, but users need local inventory, size, access, price and booking. An availability marketplace, affiliate comparison or direct operator partnership is a better product than a rented phone number.

Bark provides a low-control validation path: it advertises up to £80 for a referred project when at least one professional responds, with category-dependent payouts and programme approval. “Up to £80” is not an average CPL. It can test whether a content cohort produces qualifying projects while a direct buyer is recruited. [Bark affiliate programme](https://www.bark.com/en/gb/affiliates/)

Checkatrade sells professionals memberships from a free tier through paid plans rather than publishing a universal CPL. Bark charges professionals category-dependent credits. MyBuilder's published terms describe shortlist fees that vary with likely job size, value and location. The competitive lesson is that UK contractors already buy acquisition in several forms, but there is no honest universal statement that “a plumber pays £100 per lead.”

## Business model and unit economics

### Start with accepted leads, not rent

A new asset has no defensible rental price. It has estimates. The first commercial contract should define an accepted lead and charge only for that event.

An accepted lead should satisfy all agreed rules:

- correct service and territory;
- real person or accountable business contact;
- contactable within the defined response window;
- not a duplicate inside the agreed lookback period;
- adequate timing, access and authority;
- no obvious spam, sales solicitation or job-seeker intent;
- required consent and privacy disclosure recorded;
- call duration or form answers meet the category's qualification threshold.

After at least 90 days, calculate trailing accepted leads, buyer close rate, gross contribution per job and complaint or credit rate. Then price a territory.

One practical tenancy formula is:

```text
monthly tenant floor
= trailing monthly accepted leads
× tested value per accepted lead
× 65% to 80% volume-and-risk factor
```

If a cohort produces 15 accepted leads and a buyer has repeatedly paid £60 each, a 70% factor implies £630 per month. That is not a market benchmark; it is an example of how to translate observed performance into a fixed floor without pretending all lead risk disappears.

The preferred end state is a hybrid:

```text
monthly revenue = base territory fee + accepted leads × performance fee
```

The base funds minimum availability, data and content maintenance. The performance fee preserves alignment. The [economics scenarios](./evidence/economics-scenarios.csv) show how 8, 20 and 40 raw leads behave under explicit acceptance and pricing assumptions. They are sensitivity cases, not forecasts.

### CPC does not equal what a lead is worth

Historical CPC shows what advertisers have been willing to pay for a click in Google's auction. It does not say what a contractor will pay this publisher for a lead.

The maximum paid CPC for a lead-only arbitrage is:

```text
maximum CPC = accepted lead price × click-to-accepted-lead rate
```

If an accepted lead sells for £60 and 12% of paid clicks become accepted leads, break-even media CPC before overhead is £7.20. Buying a £27 click under those economics loses money. A £27 CPC can still be rational for a contractor that closes valuable jobs and owns the job margin; it is not automatically rational for an intermediary selling a £60 lead.

Paid search is therefore a **validation instrument**, not the assumed growth engine. It can reveal landing-page conversion, query quality and buyer response quickly, but only if a buyer or revenue-share agreement supports the spend. No campaign was created or modified in this research.

The deeper [CPC sensitivity model](./evidence/cpc-economics-sensitivity.csv) makes the constraint explicit. Its base case assumes 10% click-to-enquiry, 70% enquiry acceptance and 20% buyer close from accepted leads. On the observed local-geo weighted CPCs, that implies about £119 paid media per accepted Bristol drainage lead, £165 for Manchester fire risk, £232 for Manchester asbestos and £241 for Edinburgh boilers—before staff, call handling, credits, overhead, tax or profit. These are not forecasts. They show the performance hurdle a paid validation cohort must clear.

### Model fit by service shape

| Service shape               | Best starting model                    | Why                                                          |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Urgent phone call           | Pay per connected qualified call       | Contactability and dispatch are observable immediately       |

The Jackson Manchester website identifies company 03893399, not the initially selected related 08429080, so the records are kept as one unresolved group family rather than two buyers. Maintain-A-Drain (South West) Limited files dormant accounts while an active same-name website exists; the live trading entity was not established, so it cannot be treated as a verified buyer.

Read-only advanced Companies House searches add broad discovery bounds: 187 active Edinburgh registered-office companies under cleaning SIC 81210, 261 under plumbing/heating SIC 43220, 20 Bristol sewerage-SIC matches, 636 Bristol plumbing/heating matches, and Manchester screens of 37 fire-service-SIC and 147 technical-testing matches. Name searches are narrower: 47 Edinburgh `CLEAN`, 103 Edinburgh `HEATING`, 183 Manchester `FIRE`, six Manchester `ASBESTOS`, and ten Bristol `DRAINAGE` matches. These counts contain omissions, overlaps and false positives. They are list-building evidence, not buyer counts.

Companies House states that it does not independently verify the accuracy of company-filed information. A registry record proves neither service quality, geographic coverage nor willingness to buy leads. [Companies House quality and methods guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/incorporated-companies-in-the-uk-by-jurisdiction-and-month-quality-and-methods-guide/quality-and-methods-guide)

## Case studies: what is real and what remains marketing

Public evidence is the weakest part of the rank-and-rent thesis. No audited, representative UK portfolio benchmark was found. The visible evidence is mostly operator-authored marketing, podcast claims and individual forum reports.

### FatRank's £83,000 case

FatRank reports that an unnamed site ranked within six months and then produced 6–10 leads per day. It discloses £83,000 of build cost: 86 articles at £8,600, 207 do-follow backlinks at £41,400, five Wikipedia backlinks at £1,000, 400 tier-two links at £8,000 and £24,000 of editor wages. It reports £4,000 monthly rent. [FatRank case study](https://www.fatrank.com/rank-and-rent-case-study/)

Annual gross rent of £48,000 divided by £83,000 is a 57.8% gross yield. Simple payback is 20.75 months. Those are derived arithmetic, not profit. Maintenance, tax, sales cost, tenant churn, ranking loss and failed assets are absent. The niche, site, tenant and revenue record are hidden, so the case is useful as an operator claim and cost composition—not as an investment benchmark.

### Dooley's portfolio claim

In a 2023 Niche Pursuits interview, James Dooley described 850 paying sites across 650 niches and more than 56 million page-one rankings. He also said failures were repurposed into ads, testing or private link-network assets. [Niche Pursuits interview](https://www.nichepursuits.com/james-dooley/)

The admission of failures is informative: portfolio economics need the cost of all attempts, not only rented sites. But no asset list, failure denominator, revenue evidence or independent audit was supplied. The claim identifies a prominent UK operator; it does not establish a typical outcome.

### The Chameleon roofing example

Chameleon Overlay reports a mid-sized northern-city roofing site built for £1,400, reaching 18–22 calls a month by month six and renting for £650 a month, later £900 after passing 30 calls. [Chameleon Overlay playbook](https://chameleonoverlay.com/blog/local-seo-rank-and-rent-playbook)

Its playbook also recommends a virtual-office Google profile. That directly conflicts with current Google guidance requiring in-person customer contact and listing lead-generation companies as ineligible. The attractive economics therefore cannot be separated from a material replicability risk.

### The UK boiler failure

A UK builder documented a boiler-repair network attempt as “not especially successful.” Link building was slow, early rankings remained outside the top 100, the builder lost interest, and outreach offering free traffic to selected gas engineers received no replies. The author saw no obvious UK affiliate-lead route and later narrowed focus after the cost and delay of several sites. [UK boiler case](https://www.reddit.com/r/juststart/comments/t123be/uk_boiler_repair_network_site_rank_and_rent_case/)

This anecdote does not prove UK rank-and-rent fails. It demonstrates two independent risks that promoter cases often hide: the site may not rank, and the buyer may not materialise even if the niche looks attractive in keyword software.

### A US pay-per-call reference

Lead Smart published a 2026 US case reporting 124 inbound calls, 39 completed, 15 converted and $1,066.90 revenue over 20 days. It transparently shows weak days and the gap between dialled and completed calls. [Lead Smart case](https://leadsmartinc.com/news/case-study-rank-and-rent-20-day-report)

It is useful for funnel mechanics, not UK economics. It comes from a US affiliate network with a dense buyer and payout infrastructure that the UK operator cannot assume exists.

## Who is talking about the model

The conversation has four groups with different incentives.

**UK operators and educators.** James Dooley and FatRank are the most visible high-scale advocates found. Charles Floate interviews and discusses the model within the same ecosystem. Craig Riley and Chameleon Overlay publish local playbooks and software-led tactics. RankAndRent.uk sells a low-priced course. Leadrent presents an operating UK lead-generation offer. These sources reveal terminology, tactics and sales objections, but their income is helped when the model appears attractive.

**International educators and publishers.** Jase Rodley, Luke Van Der Veer, Ippei Kanehara and rank-and-rent podcast or community personalities supply much of the public blueprint. US advice often assumes easier access to pay-per-call networks, a larger contractor market and different local-search competition. It cannot be imported without a UK buyer map.

**Software and network vendors.** RankAndRentHQ, Chameleon Overlay, Lead Smart and other call-routing or overlay products benefit when more sites enter the category. Their case studies can be operationally detailed but are selected, not representative.

**Search and local-marketing researchers.** Google, Pew, Ahrefs, Whitespark and BrightLocal supply the evidence needed to challenge the tactics. Google is authoritative on its rules but commercially interested in its own search narrative. The independent and vendor studies have useful methods but mostly US or informational-query samples.

The correct reading discipline is to ask of every claim: who supplied it, what do they sell, is the site or revenue identifiable, are failed attempts included, and can the mechanism transfer to a UK market?

## AI Overviews and AI Mode

Google launched AI Mode in the UK on 28 July 2025. Google says early users ask questions two to three times longer than traditional searches and that AI Mode uses query fan-out across subtopics. [Google UK AI Mode](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/around-the-globe/google-europe/united-kingdom/ai-mode-search-uk/)

Independent evidence points to click compression. Pew analysed 68,879 searches by 900 US adults in March 2025. When an AI summary appeared, users clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits, versus 15% without one; summary-source links received clicks in 1% of visits. Sessions ended on 26% of pages with a summary versus 16% without. Longer and question-form queries triggered summaries more often. [Pew click study](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)

Ahrefs compared 150,000 AI Overview keywords with 150,000 informational controls and estimated that an Overview correlated with a 58% lower position-one CTR in December 2025. It is a vendor study and primarily about informational keywords, not UK local action queries. [Ahrefs CTR update](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/)

The most relevant local study is Whitespark's 540-query sample across six industries and three US cities. It found AI Overviews for 15% of direct local-intent queries, 92% of informational queries and 97% of hybrid queries. Local packs appeared for 93%, 6% and 17% respectively. [Whitespark local AI study](https://whitespark.ca/blog/case-study-the-prevalence-of-ai-overviews-in-local-search/)

The geography does not transfer mechanically to Britain, but the intent split is strategically useful:

- **Direct action:** `drain unblocking Bristol` is relatively more resilient because the user needs a provider, availability and contact.
- **Informational:** `how to unblock a drain` is easy for an answer engine to satisfy without a click.
- **Hybrid commercial:** `how much does drain unblocking cost in Bristol` is especially exposed because AI can synthesise prices while still influencing provider selection.

The response is not to abandon information. It is to make information feed a transaction and contain original facts. A cost page should show the actual price structure, arrival windows, included work, recent local quote ranges, shared-drain responsibility, emergency premiums and provider availability. A compliance page should expose credential checks, premises types, report turnaround and accountable providers.

AI also creates a brand-verification problem. BrightLocal's 2026 US panel found 23% used AI in their most recent local search, but only 18% of AI users were ready to contact the recommended business without more verification. The sample is US-based, yet it supports a sensible design principle: consistent provider identity, reviews, registrations and contact facts across the web matter because AI is a shortlist layer rather than the only decision surface. [BrightLocal channels study](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/consumer-search-behavior-channels/)

Measurement has improved. Google announced that as of 31 August 2026 all websites had access to dedicated Search Console reports showing generative-AI impressions, pages, countries, devices and dates. Every launched asset should baseline and monitor that report separately from conventional clicks and leads. [Google generative AI reports](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports)

## What wins in organic search now

The winning unit is a **useful local service transaction**, not an SEO page template.

For each vertical, build one canonical service and qualification schema. A fire-risk lead needs premises use, occupancy, sleeping risk and prior assessment. A house-clearance lead needs volume, photos and disposal constraints. A drain call needs symptoms, shared-drain status and urgency. Those fields generate unique user value and structured operational data.

Each public location should exist because the operation differs there. Valid differences include provider coverage, response window, local authority or water-company responsibilities, property stock, price observations, certifications, case evidence and appointment availability. A city name inserted into otherwise identical copy is not a local product.

The minimum trust surface is:

- clear publisher and commercial-intermediary identity;
- named provider before or at the point of handoff;
- what happens to the enquiry and who may contact the user;
- independently checkable credentials where relevant;
- original project or service evidence with dates and locations at a sensible granularity;
- explicit price drivers rather than invented “from” figures;
- real answer and response-time standards;
- review provenance and a policy against selective fabrication;
- corrections, complaints and deletion routes.

AI can assist research, call summarisation, entity extraction, quality checks, internal linking and draft structure. It should not manufacture local experience, reviews, jobs, credentials or hundreds of near-duplicate pages. Google states that using generative AI to create many pages without added value can be scaled-content abuse. [Google people-first guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

## Acquisition and operations

The sequence begins with supply and buyers, not keywords.

For a candidate market, identify at least 20 plausible fulfillment businesses. Check coverage, credentials, review depth, response hours, service mix and visible growth signals. Speak to ten. The research question is not “would you like more leads?” It is:

- Which jobs do you want and reject?
- Which postcodes can you serve profitably?
- What makes a lead unworkable?
- How quickly can you answer and attend?
- What did you pay for acquisition last quarter?
- What is a won job worth in gross contribution, not revenue?
- What share of qualified enquiries become surveys, quotes and wins?
- What weekly capacity can you accept?
- Would you trial exclusive accepted leads with a written credit policy?

Secure at least two trial buyers before producing a large content estate. One buyer creates concentration risk and can strand an asset.

The lead system should maintain a lifecycle:

```text
visit → contact attempt → connected → qualified → accepted
→ appointment or quote → won or lost → revenue band → complaint or credit
```

Every rejection needs a reason code. Every buyer needs an answer-time metric. Call recordings, where used, need an appropriate notice and retention policy. Forms should collect only fields needed for qualification. Sensitive details should not be gathered because they might be useful later.

The dashboard should separate:

- organic page and query;
- conventional Search versus generative-AI visibility;
- direct local, informational and hybrid intent;
- raw, connected, qualified and accepted leads;
- buyer answer time and acceptance;
- appointment, quote and win rate;
- gross contribution band where the buyer shares it;
- credit, duplicate and complaint rates;
- revenue per accepted lead and per organic landing session.

SEO success is not ranking. It is positive cohort contribution with stable buyer satisfaction.

## UK compliance and trust

The site must disclose that it generates or introduces leads. CAP guidance says marketing must make commercial intent clear and cites comparison-style lead sites that were misleading because users were not told prominently that their details would be passed to providers. A footer disclaimer cannot repair a page whose overall impression is “we are the contractor” or “we independently compare the market” when neither is true. [ASA/CAP lead-generation guidance](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/lead-generation-marketing.html)

At the form, use a just-in-time explanation: who operates the site, why the information is collected, which named provider or clearly described provider class will receive it, what contact follows and where the full privacy notice sits. The ICO says people must receive privacy information when details are collected for direct marketing and organisations buying or renting information remain responsible for due diligence, lawful basis and preferences. [ICO lead collection guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/collect-information-and-generate-leads/)

A requested quote routed to a provider for that request is not automatically identical to unrelated future direct marketing. The exact lawful basis and PECR position depend on the workflow. Do not use a blanket “consent solves everything” statement; document each purpose and obtain specialist review for the live design.

Category controls include:

- fire risk assessment: route only to competent assessors for the premises type and state accurately when a third party is needed;
- asbestos: verify relevant competence and accreditation claims; do not blur independent survey and removal incentives;
- gas boilers: verify Gas Safe business and engineer scope;
- house clearance and skip hire: verify the relevant national waste-carrier register;
- mortgages and financial products: assume FCA financial-promotion and introducer scrutiny until expert advice confirms the perimeter;
- personal injury, health and funerals: apply heightened vulnerability, advertising and data controls or exclude them from launch.

This report does not provide legal advice.

## Ninety-day validation plan

### Days 1–15: prove buyer reality

Run buyer discovery across the four deeper-screen survivors, then select at most two validation lanes. I would use fire-risk assessment Manchester for a premises-qualified B2B appointment and Bristol drainage for a fast, operationally measurable call funnel. Keep asbestos in partner discovery because of its strong CPC agreement, and boilers in buyer discovery because of its high order value. Build a buyer ledger, conduct ten conversations per selected market and secure two written trial arrangements per market before a production build.

Stop if fewer than two credible buyers will define an accepted lead, share rejection reasons and commit to response standards. Do not treat polite interest as demand.

### Days 16–30: build the qualification product

Create one service schema, one primary landing experience and one evidence-rich city page per market. Publish the intermediary disclosure, privacy flow, provider-verification standard and lead-credit policy. Instrument calls, forms, source, page, response time and outcome.

### Days 31–60: validate acquisition carefully

Use direct partner traffic, small disclosed marketplace tests, outreach to relevant local organisations, original data and earned citations. A bounded paid-search test can be justified only where the buyer economics support the observed CPC. No campaign operation was performed in this research.

Do not bulk-publish city pages. Earn the second city by proving that the first page generates accepted leads and by collecting unique operational evidence.

### Days 61–90: price the cohort

Measure raw-to-connected, connected-to-accepted, response time, appointment, quote, win, credit and complaint rates. Continue accepted-lead billing. A fixed tenancy remains premature unless lead flow is stable and buyer close economics are known.

At day 90:

- **Go:** at least two buyers, repeat accepted leads, an agreed price above delivery cost, reliable response and no material compliance failure.
- **Revise:** leads exist but buyer response, qualification or pricing is weak.
- **Stop:** the site produces low-intent contacts, buyers reject the cohort, the economics require paid CPC above break-even or the only ranking route depends on ineligible profiles or deceptive presentation.

## Twelve-month build

Months 4–6 should deepen one vertical rather than add unrelated trades. Fire-risk assessment might expand from Manchester to Leeds or Birmingham with local competent-provider coverage. Drainage should expand only after connected-call response and contribution are proven. Commercial cleaning remains paused until its Edinburgh query anomaly is resolved. The data indicates where to inspect; it does not grant permission to replicate.

Months 7–9 should add the hybrid contract, provider scorecards and user-facing availability or price evidence. Create original quarterly market data: median response window, quoted price drivers, acceptance reasons and verified coverage. Aggregate only where privacy and sample size permit.

Months 10–12 should decide whether the company remains a lead publisher, becomes a vertical marketplace or enters an owned-operator partnership. Expand to London only if the conversion and buyer-contribution model can tolerate its competition.

A sensible year-one portfolio is six to twelve strong territories inside one or two verticals, not hundreds of sites. The objective is a repeatable accepted-lead engine with buyer redundancy and transparent unit economics.

## Final investment case

The case for the business is strong where five facts coincide:

1. explicit local or near-me demand is meaningful;
2. CPC indicates commercial competition but not so much that validation is impossible;
3. a won job has enough contribution to fund acquisition;
4. multiple credible local providers want the same defined work;
5. the site can add transaction value without a fake location, fake provider or scaled doorway estate.

The research finds several such candidates, especially competent-person surveys and selected high-value or urgent local trades outside London. It does not find evidence that passive, low-cost, hands-off digital real estate is a dependable UK asset class. The strongest public success case spent £83,000. The strongest UK failure anecdote could not rank or recruit buyers. Google has closed off speculative lead-generator profiles. AI is compressing generic informational clicks.

That is not the death of the model. It is the removal of the easy story.

The investable version is an operator-led acquisition company: buyer-first, transparent, measured at the accepted-lead and won-job level, and built around original local operating data. If we pursue it, I would begin commercial discovery in Manchester fire and asbestos, Bristol drainage and Edinburgh boilers; select only the strongest one or two buyer-backed lanes for acquisition; and refuse to scale until those cohorts prove contribution. Edinburgh cleaning should not return to the queue until its search anomaly is independently resolved.

## What remains unknown

The most important unknowns require real commercial work rather than more desk research:

- actual buyer willingness to pay by accepted-lead definition;
- available weekly capacity and geographic coverage;
- close rate and gross contribution by job type;
- true local-pack composition from target postcodes and devices;
- Search Console click and generative-AI visibility once pages are live;
- link and content cost required for each result set;
- seasonality at a finer level than rounded Google Ads averages;
- complaint, credit and duplicate rates;
- whether marketplace programmes accept the exact traffic method;
- legal review of the final disclosure, call-recording and data-sharing workflow.

These are not footnotes. They are the gates between an attractive demand screen and an investable business.
