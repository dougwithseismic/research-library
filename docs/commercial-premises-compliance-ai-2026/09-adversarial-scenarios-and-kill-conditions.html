Adversarial Scenarios and Kill Conditions

Incumbent response, agent mediation, trust shocks, data failure and explicit stop conditions.

Geography
United Kingdom
Research date
2026-09-03
Status
Published research

What is the strongest case against the thesis?#

The strongest case is that there is no missing company between property teams, existing software and specialist providers. Buyers already use spreadsheets, shared drives, facilities systems and managing agents. Providers already schedule, inspect and report. Horizontal platforms are adding document AI and agents. A new managed layer could become expensive clerical work with ambiguous liability and no durable willingness to pay.

The validation programme must try to prove that case, not protect the original recommendation.

Scenario one: AI adoption remains limited#

Models improve extraction and drafting, but safety concerns, fragmented documents and conservative buyers prevent broad autonomous use. Most workflows remain human led.

Effect#

The company still has a service opportunity if evidence fragmentation and action follow-through are valuable. Its software margin develops more slowly, and expert operations dominate cost.

Winning response#

Sell the managed outcome without requiring customers to believe an AI story. Use models internally only where measured. Price human exception work honestly.

Failure signal#

The service cannot produce positive site contribution without assuming future automation, or customers refuse the recurring fee once onboarding is complete.

Scenario two: agent-mediated compliance becomes normal#

Customer workplace agents answer obligation questions, collect documents, compare providers and schedule work through APIs.

Effect#

Generic content and lead forms lose value. A directory or referral page can be bypassed. The managed operator may receive demand through an agent rather than a website.

Winning response#

Expose permissioned premises state, evidence requirements, provider options and service events through stable structured interfaces. Charge for the managed site or completed workflow rather than a human click. Keep human approval and audit for consequential actions.

Failure signal#

Providers expose complete scope, credentials, capacity and completion evidence directly to agents, while property systems already hold the customer's trusted record and no party pays for the intermediary.

Scenario three: horizontal incumbents bundle the product#

Property and maintenance platforms add compliance calendars, credential checks, evidence extraction, anomaly detection and provider agents to existing subscriptions.

Effect#

Standalone software price and feature differentiation collapse. Incumbents have integrations, assets and user permissions that a startup would need years to reproduce.

Winning response#

Operate as a specialist service or embedded provider network on top of those systems. Own technical authority, evidence quality, provider fulfilment and action closure rather than generic records.

Failure signal#

Target buyers demonstrate that their current platform and facilities provider already deliver the exact cross-lane workflow with low missed work, portable evidence and no painful service gap.

Scenario four: vertical providers absorb the coordination layer#

Fire, asbestos, water-safety and engineering firms improve customer portals, action tracking and cross-sell. Regional providers consolidate.

Effect#

The customer may prefer one trusted service company over an independent coordinator. The entrant struggles to obtain provider margin or data rights.

Winning response#

Serve multi-site buyers that need independence across several lane-specific providers, or supply provider-neutral infrastructure. Keep referral conflicts visible.

Failure signal#

Customers consistently buy a bundled facilities contract, and independent coordination neither improves price nor reduces missed actions.

Scenario five: unsafe automation creates a trust shock#

A generated report invents observations, a credential matcher approves the wrong person, or an agent closes incomplete work. Customers, insurers or regulators respond by restricting AI-assisted workflows.

Effect#

Model access and output may become more constrained. Review and audit costs rise. Firms that marketed autonomous certification suffer severe trust loss.

Winning response#

Keep models outside sign-off, retain original evidence and named approvals, publish the control and correction method, and remain able to operate manually. The system's value should be provenance rather than autonomy theatre.

Failure signal#

Insurers or professional partners will not support the operating model, or competent review cost removes the economic gain.

Scenario six: regulation changes the verification layer#

The proposed fire-risk-assessor competence regime and central register becomes law, or its design changes. The 2031 EPC B policy intention becomes secondary legislation, changes again or does not proceed. Fire-assessor consultation MEES interim response

Effect#

Provider discovery can become easier, competence requirements can raise supply costs, and obligation rules can change. A static content library decays.

Winning response#

Version sources and rules, keep proposals separate from current law, integrate authoritative registers and make change review part of the service.

Failure signal#

The company cannot maintain qualified technical authority or keeps presenting superseded policy as a current duty.

Scenario seven: customers capture all AI savings#

Report and administration time fall across the industry. Competition passes the saving into lower prices instead of higher provider or platform margin.

Effect#

Per-assessment revenue declines and AI features stop supporting a premium. A company valued on gross hours saved disappoints.

Winning response#

Use lower delivery cost to broaden managed coverage, improve turnaround and retain customers. Charge for recurring evidence quality and reduced failures, not for access to a model.

Failure signal#

The managed operator cannot retain any productivity benefit and the recurring coordination outcome has no separate willingness to pay.

Scenario eight: the data moat never forms#

Customers will not share original evidence, providers deliver inconsistent PDFs, identifiers fail to match, contracts block reuse and the operation cannot observe remediation outcomes.

Effect#

The proposed graph remains a document folder. Models cannot improve safely and the company has no learning advantage over a new entrant.

Winning response#

Make evidence export and structured delivery part of customer and provider terms, define a minimal site and asset identity standard, and narrow to lanes with usable records.

Failure signal#

After 50 sites, critical fields still lack provenance, actions cannot be linked to outcomes and no permitted data improves scoping, quality or provider selection.

Which assumptions deserve the hardest attack?#

“Every site needs recurring external assessments”#

False. Some duties allow competent internal action, and review is often risk- or event-based. The product must earn recurring revenue from evidence and follow-through rather than invent a yearly purchase.

“A large property stock means a large reachable market”#

False. Rateable properties are not buyers. Dutyholder, trigger, existing contract, asset and budget must be resolved before a site enters the serviceable market.

“AI can complete the report, so it can complete the work”#

False. A fluent report can be generated without valid observation, competence, remediation or sign-off. Output quality and work completion are separate.

“More alerts improve compliance”#

False. Poor thresholds create alert fatigue and can hide the critical signal. Anomaly models need site-specific baselines, precision, recall and human disposition.

“A human reviewer makes the workflow safe”#

False without evidence. The reviewer needs competence, source visibility, time, authority and measurable behaviour. Automation bias can turn a nominal review into a rubber stamp.

“Public credentials solve provider quality”#

False. A register can support identity and scope. It does not prove current capacity, service, report quality, response or customer outcome.

“An acquired operator automatically supplies a data moat”#

False. Historical records may be poor, legally restricted, incomplete or attached to a departing owner. Data rights and usability need diligence.

What are the immediate kill conditions?#

Stop the initial launch if:

  • fewer than five of eight property-manager interviews describe the same expensive recurring workflow with usable evidence;
  • no named budget owner will pay for or delegate evidence coordination;
  • no professional reviewer will approve the obligation and escalation rules;
  • fewer than two qualified providers per live lane and region accept the service, data and responsibility terms;
  • provider contribution cannot carry acquisition and coordination under a conservative case;
  • no two paid portfolio design partners retain for 90 days;
  • human service cost consumes recurring site contribution;
  • any live AI workflow produces an unauthorised sign-off, fabricated critical observation, critical credential error, wrong critical jurisdiction or confirmed false closure;
  • sensitive customer or building data is disclosed without authority;
  • the existing customer stack solves the measured problem without switching value.

What should narrow rather than kill the thesis?#

Narrow to one lane if cross-lane rules and provider operations add cost without retention. Narrow to evidence cleanup if buyers value onboarding but not ongoing management. Serve providers instead of dutyholders if assessor administration has stronger willingness to pay. Become an implementation partner to an incumbent if its platform owns the record but lacks lane-specific setup. Abandon acquisition while retaining partnership if historic liability cannot be priced.

The decision should follow observed contribution and safety, not attachment to a product form.

What would count as genuine upside?#

Upside requires all of the following, not merely faster text generation:

  • at least two paid design partners and 50 managed sites;
  • a repeated evidence or action problem across customer types;
  • two-provider redundancy per live lane and region;
  • measurable accepted provider work or avoided failure;
  • positive site contribution after human exceptions;
  • at least 20% reviewer-time improvement without safety regression;
  • zero critical AI events under the stated thresholds;
  • customer-authorised longitudinal data that improves an operating decision;
  • retention beyond the initial evidence cleanup;
  • a route to scale that does not depend on misleading universal obligations.

The adversarial decision#

The thesis survives desk scrutiny only as a test. It does not survive as a claim that every premises must buy recurring certificates, that AI can certify buildings or that an aggregator can keep the full productivity gain. The validation programme should be willing to convert the idea into a smaller service, an incumbent partnership or a clear decision to remain out.

This publication distinguishes observed evidence, derived calculations, commercial inference and unknowns.
