Event-Format City Depth and Fill-Sensitive Economics

A 15-city, 24-format demand comparison with operator prices, fill-sensitive economics, partner constraints and paid pilot gates.

Geography
UK; selected European and US comparators
Research date
2026-09-03
Status
Published research

The answer#

The deeper screen strengthens the case for darts, social dance, hosted games, portable making and producer-led tasting, but it changes how they should be launched.

The best founder-stage sequence is:

  1. social darts — the best all-round paid pilot;
  2. hosted board games — strong social structure, but only with a premium host layer rather than commodity game access;
  3. salsa or line dancing — the best repeat-purchase engine;
  4. pottery painting — exceptionally broad demand, but the event must use partner capacity and clear adult positioning;
  5. wine tasting — attractive partner economics and price depth, with weaker repeat by default;
  6. sip and paint or another portable workshop — easy to stage, but differentiation and repeat still need proof.

Karaoke and padel have enormous visible demand. They are not automatic company wedges. Much of that demand is for a karaoke room, venue or padel court that already exists. Pottery classes and social sport have good recurring structures but become fragile when equipment, firing or facility costs force high fill. Yoga and spa retreats have premium ticket potential, but they should be extensions of an audience and partner network, not the first product.

For a non-London launch, the strongest cells are not identical:

  • Manchester: darts, wine, cooking and a higher-pressure proof market;
  • Birmingham: pottery painting, board games, dance and sip-and-paint;
  • Bristol: pottery, board games, nature and the strongest low-capital retreat-prevalidation cell;
  • Glasgow: darts, line dancing and quiz-led formats;
  • Liverpool: pottery, darts and portable art;
  • Edinburgh: Dungeons & Dragons and premium cultural or seasonal formats.

The immediate recommendation is one six-event darts or dance series in Manchester or Birmingham, with one workshop counter-test, not a multi-format national calendar.

What this deeper screen added#

The national map covered 244 formats. This stage selected 24 formats that represent the leading activity types, the user's explicit interest in workshops and spa retreats, and several high-demand substitutes that could otherwise distort the answer.

It then added:

  • 15 verified UK city targets: London, Birmingham, Manchester, Leeds, Bristol, Nottingham, Southampton, Reading, Edinburgh, Glasgow, Liverpool, Sheffield, Newcastle, Cardiff and Belfast;
  • 360 explicit city-modified keyword observations under the UK target;
  • 360 generic near me observations inside individual city targets;
  • a city-format liquidity comparison that keeps the two methods separate;
  • a 24-format deep score with a decision ceiling for facility, fulfilment and substitute risk;
  • twelve current operator and price benchmarks;
  • 24 fill-sensitive economics rows across eight delivery models.

The city IDs were checked against Google's 12 August 2026 geo-target file. Every retained target is a current Active UK City; Newcastle uses the English target, not the same-named Northern Irish city. Google Ads geo-target data

The raw files are the explicit-city screen, local-geo screen, 360-cell summary, verified geo targets, deep scorecard, operator benchmarks and fill-sensitivity model.

Read the city numbers correctly#

The two keyword methods answer different questions.

darts Manchester under the UK target measures explicit use of Manchester in the query. darts near me inside the Manchester city target measures a generic local-discovery phrase associated with that target. They overlap. They must not be added.

The comparative city-format index gives each method half of the weight after log normalisation. This prevents one very large term from overwhelming every smaller format, but it remains a ranking device rather than market size. A score of 72 does not mean that 72% of a market exists or will convert.

There are three other important distortions:

  1. a city target is an administrative boundary, not a metro catchment or safe late-evening travel zone;
  2. near me activity terms mix adult, child, family, tourist, retail and facility intent;
  3. retreat demand is often destination-led, so a city keyword screen systematically understates a product sold around accommodation, landscape or a practitioner audience.

The 24-format deep ranking#

The deeper score uses national demand, best-city liquidity, breadth across the 15 cities, commercial intent, repeatability, social design, asset-light control, current price evidence and eventisation headroom. The full formula and every component are in the retained scorecard.

A decision ceiling then prevents a high-volume facility query from becoming an unconditional launch recommendation. Padel, social sport, pottery courses and retreats are partner-only even when their weighted score is high. Karaoke, running and several instructor-led formats require focused validation because cheap or free substitutes are material.

What the city cells say#

Manchester is the clearest darts challenger#

darts Manchester returned 2,900 average monthly searches, the same rounded result as darts London. The local-geo observations differ sharply: 320 for Manchester versus 3,600 for London. This disagreement is useful. It says Manchester is explicitly associated with darts in national search behaviour but has a much smaller administrative local pool.

Manchester also leads the non-London cells for wine tasting, cooking classes, karaoke and padel. It is the best pressure test for a differentiated activity company, not the easiest place for an undifferentiated night.

Birmingham has the broadest useful activity shape#

Birmingham is the leading non-London cell for pottery painting, board-game cafés, salsa, line dancing, sip-and-paint, chess, volleyball and netball in this basket. Its pottery signal is particularly large: 4,400 explicit and 880 local-geo average monthly searches.

That is not proof of adult social-event whitespace. Pottery-painting searches include family and retail intent. It does make Birmingham the strongest place to test whether an explicitly adult, solo-friendly making product can convert existing category awareness.

Bristol is a practical counter-test#

Bristol ties Birmingham on board-game cafés at an index of 63 and remains strong for pottery painting at 73. It also leads the non-London nature-walk and yoga-retreat cells, though the retreat observations are small.

The city is valuable because it can test whether a format works outside the largest demand pools without immediately falling into a tiny market. A Bristol result should be judged on conversion and repeat rather than absolute search totals.

Glasgow has a distinctive dance-and-quiz shape#

Glasgow is level with Birmingham on line dancing at an index of 56 and leads the non-London quiz-night cells at 54. It is also third for darts behind Manchester. This suggests a useful transfer market after a format has worked once, especially for a programme that can travel through venue groups and trained hosts.

Belfast speed dating is an anomaly, not a recommendation#

speed dating Belfast returned 880 average monthly searches while several larger cities returned no reportable explicit volume for the selected phrase. The local Belfast observation was only ten. The result could reflect real demand, event-brand language, close-variant behaviour or a temporary historical pattern.

It deserves a small landing-page and inventory test. It does not justify treating Belfast as a proven category leader.

The observed price ladder#

Current operator evidence shows what customers can already buy, not the margin available to a new organiser.

  • Flight Club lists Social Darts for groups of 2–36 from £9 per person, brunch from £33 and a separate 37+ route. Flight Club
  • Draughts lists board-game admission at £7.50 off-peak and £9.50 peak for up to three hours, with staff available to teach games. Draughts
  • Salsateca lists a one-hour class at £15, two hours at £17, three sessions for £47, six for £85 and subscriptions from £48 to £130. Salsateca
  • Southwark's adult-learning provision lists ten-week pottery courses at £220–£264 plus a £5 annual registration fee. The effective listed course price is £22–£26.40 per session before registration, but subsidised council provision is not a private margin benchmark. Southwark Council
  • A selected GO Mammoth basketball league lists an individual weekly equivalent of £12.20 across an eight-to-twelve-week league. It demonstrates the season model, not volleyball or netball economics in every city. GO Mammoth
  • The Wine Society currently runs multiple tasting types across UK cities. Calendar listings and availability states demonstrate inventory, not attendance or contribution. The Wine Society
  • Emyoga lists a three-day, two-night retreat at £395 shared or £495 single with a £150 deposit; Zest Life exposes a higher premium tier. Emyoga, Zest Life
  • Macdonald Hotels lists half-day spa days from £64 and full days from £85. A hosted retreat must create value above that asset-only substitute. Macdonald Hotels

This ladder supports three conclusions. First, a portable hosted event cannot simply charge the established venue price while paying retail for the venue. Second, course and pack mechanics are more important than a higher first ticket. Third, retreats have enough ticket depth to cover programming, but only if wholesale inventory, cancellation and fill are controlled.

The table reveals the operational difference between an attractive category and an attractive first event.

Pottery and social sport can build excellent recurring products, yet the illustrative first-session economics are nearly binary: they need high fill because materials, firing and facility capacity are committed before customer repeat is known. A spa day looks safer on percentage fill, but its absolute cash commitment and partner dependency are much larger. The residential retreat produces only £56 of direct contribution at 70% fill in this scenario; one refund, rooming change or acquisition miss could remove it.

Darts and wine have the best balance of break-even fill and useful contribution in the model. Dance becomes attractive when the event sells packs or subscriptions, because a single 70%-full night produces too little contribution to carry central acquisition and product costs. Board games require either better venue terms, higher yield, secondary spend participation or a repeat product; commodity admission alone is not enough.

What I would actually test#

Test one: Manchester social darts#

Run six consecutive weekday sessions in one pub. Sell the season as a beginner-friendly ladder with rotating small teams, standings and a final rather than six disconnected tickets.

Management gates, derived from the scenario rather than market benchmarks:

  • secure terms that keep direct break-even below 60% fill;
  • reach at least 70% paid fill by event three without a permanent discount;
  • achieve at least 20% direct event contribution by event four;
  • have at least 30% of first-time attendees place a second paid booking or season deposit within 45 days;
  • keep host-dependent complaints and unresolved safety incidents low enough that the format can be delivered by a trained non-founder host.

Test two: Birmingham social dance#

Test line dancing and salsa as two landing pages before choosing one. The event should include instruction, rotation, practice and a clear pack offer. The key result is not first-night attendance; it is whether customers buy the next four-to-six sessions.

Proceed only if one proposition produces paid demand at the full intended price and at least 30% of first attendees buy or deposit on the next block. Stop if the event is enjoyable but behaves like a one-off night out.

Counter-test: Birmingham or Bristol pottery painting#

Use spare studio capacity or outsourced firing. Do not sign a lease or buy a kiln. The present scenario requires roughly 78% fill, so obtain enough paid bookings before the cancellation deadline to cover the direct break-even attendance.

If the event cannot command £45, improve wholesale terms or remove the format from the first portfolio. Search volume does not repair a structurally thin session.

Retreat prevalidation: Bristol audience, destination product#

Do not use yoga retreats Bristol as the complete demand test. Build a concrete two-night proposition with named accommodation, practitioner, room tiers, cancellation schedule and travel detail. Collect a small number of clearly refundable deposits before committing inventory.

The retreat becomes a launch candidate only after city events or a practitioner audience provide a reusable buyer pool. Cold paid search into high working capital is the wrong first proof.

The portfolio rule#

One flagship should create the relationship; one depth product should monetise repeat.

Flagship Depth product Why the pair works
Social darts night Six-week league Low-anxiety trial becomes progression and team identity
Dance social Class pack or beginner block Improvement and familiarity reward return
Hosted board-game night Campaign, ladder or membership Discovery becomes continuity
Pottery taster Bounded project course A finished object leads to skill progression
Wine tasting Themed series or club Producer and region themes create a calendar
City wellness event Partner retreat Low-commitment trust precedes high-ticket inventory

The company should not own a darts venue, pottery studio, kitchen, sports facility or spa until repeated paid cohorts show that owning the asset would improve contribution more than it increases fixed-cost risk.

Stop and go rules#

After six paid events, continue a format only if most of the following are true:

  • direct break-even can be held at or below 65% fill for a portable format;
  • full-price paid fill reaches 70% without relying on the facilitator's personal audience every time;
  • at least 30% of new attendees make or reserve a second purchase within 45 days;
  • direct contribution exceeds 20% before central overhead by the fourth repeat;
  • the venue, host or practitioner can be replaced without collapsing demand;
  • the organiser retains permissioned customer relationships and is not merely supplying a venue's calendar;
  • the adult, solo-friendly customer job is visible in booking answers and observed attendance rather than inferred from search volume.

Stop, redesign or move to a partner-only model if break-even requires near sell-out, the product competes mainly on facility access, repeat stays below 20%, refunds or no-shows erase contribution, or every city requires founder-led reinvention.

These gates are intentionally demanding. A busy room is not yet a company. The worthwhile format is the one that converts search language into paid attendance, paid return and a delivery system that can survive outside the founder's hands.

What remains unknown#

The deeper research reduces category and city uncertainty. It does not establish:

  • organic or map-pack difficulty for any city-format query;
  • conversion from search to an adult, solo-friendly booking;
  • actual venue, practitioner, insurance, licensing, cancellation or wholesale terms;
  • event-level paid acquisition cost;
  • attendee demographics, safety incidents, no-show or refund rates;
  • first-to-second paid booking by format and city;
  • the price elasticity of a hosted layer above a venue or free-community substitute;
  • operator profit, utilisation or available partner capacity;
  • whether the founder has an audience, venue or facilitator advantage in a specific city.

Those fields now matter more than another hundred keywords. The next evidence should come from comparable venue quotes, paid landing-page tests, customer reconstruction interviews and six delivered events in one city.
