Where to Play: Customer Segments and Scorecard

A weighted comparison of ten friendship, activity, dating, programme and platform archetypes.

Geography
UK; selected European and US comparators
Research date
2026-09-03
Status
Published research

The decision#

The scorecard compares ten friendship, dating, talks, course, games, sport, craft and discovery archetypes. The wider field changes the winner.

Ten models were scored from one to five against the method in the research chapter. The scores remain heuristic decision aids rather than empirical measurements.

Rank Archetype Score / 100 Decision
1 Interest-led regional event house + depth formats 85 Recommended launch
2 Recurring activity cohort or season 82 Strong launch model
3 Vertical activity network + supplier tools 81 Strong later or funded model
4 Touring intellectual events + media 79 Strong launch model
5 Regional hosted friendship dining, 35–65 78 Recommended depth format
6 Metro algorithmic friendship subscription 76 Strong scaled model; wrong starting state
7 Large singles mixer brand 70 Monetisable but crowded and risk-intensive
8 Multi-category local discovery app 68 Distribution or funded platform route
9 Dating app plus IRL events 64 Valuable to an incumbent
10 Open social-event marketplace 58 Reject as launch wedge

The result does not predict company size. It asks which model best combines legible demand, repeat potential, contribution, portability, differentiation, operating ease and controllable trust risk.

This model score is now paired with a separate city opportunity scorecard. The archetype ranking answers what should be built? The city screen answers where should the first evidence be bought? Keeping them separate avoids pretending that a strong format automatically has the same acquisition, venue and competitive conditions in every place.

The retained scorecard also has an explicit evidence boundary: public sources support demand signals, current formats and price anchors, but no archetype has first-party acquisition, retention, contribution and incident cohorts for the proposed company. A lead of two or three points is therefore not a meaningful empirical difference. The decision bands and first reversible test matter more than the decimal-free rank.

Recommended: interest-led event house with a depth engine#

The public acquisition product should be a concrete night people value independently: a lecture, reading party, social game, portable making class or selected sport session. Solo attendance is designed into arrival, seating and hosting.

The retention product should be smaller and repeated: a dinner, salon, short course, campaign, team or cohort derived from the public programme.

interesting public night
→ comfortable solo attendance
→ related small-group invitation
→ repeated faces
→ season, pack or capped membership

This scores above dinner alone because one large event can carry more acquisition and because the activity provides fallback value. It scores above a generic marketplace because the company owns the format and room.

Who to serve first#

The adult 35–65 regional audience is attractive, but it should be defined by behaviour rather than only age.

Priority customers:

  • people who want a better weekday evening but do not want nightlife centred on clubs;
  • adults who arrive alone and value a hosted start;
  • newcomers, remote workers, recently separated people and empty nesters rebuilding local routine;
  • intellectually or creatively curious customers who already buy classes, talks, books, games or food experiences;
  • people willing to travel 20–35 minutes for a distinctive recurring programme;
  • employers and local organisations that later need a reliable group version.

Age-guided sessions may improve comfort, but every boundary fragments supply. The programme can publish age or life-stage guidance where it affects the room without making the entire brand a demographic label.

Why activity improves demand legibility#

Friendship is the desired long-run outcome but often an awkward purchase label. Activity terms already have strong discovery language. The second keyword pull returned 40,500 UK average monthly searches for pottery painting near me, 49,500 for padel near me, 12,100 for board game cafe and 4,400 for book club near me. These queries contain mixed intent and cannot be added into a market total. They show that customers understand the objects of participation.

The activity also partially selects the audience. A history lecture and a beginner pottery workshop will produce different rooms without collecting a hundred profile attributes.

Recurring cohorts: the strongest cash-efficient alternative#

A six-week class, four-session game campaign, seasonal league or three-event salon series creates repeated contact without an indefinite subscription.

Advantages:

  • known future capacity and facilitator schedule;
  • upfront or instalment cash;
  • less weekly reacquisition;
  • natural progression and graduation;
  • stronger relationship formation through repeated exposure;
  • a clean decision point for the next cohort.

Risks:

  • refunds and make-up sessions;
  • one member's departure affects the group;
  • future delivery liability;
  • facilitator quality is concentrated;
  • a long commitment can reduce first purchase.

The initial product can therefore use a one-off public event as trial and a bounded cohort as the core repeat offer.

Touring intellectual events: a strong independent business#

Lectures on Tap and Seed Talks show that expert programming can fill larger rooms, create a direct audience and support access, online, recordings, courses and professional education. A regional operator can bootstrap with one repeat venue and a three-night season.

This model may become an excellent event and media company even if it never produces deep friendship. That is an important strategic option: the activity business does not have to fail because the relationship layer is weaker than expected.

Regional friendship dining: repositioned, not rejected#

Dinners remain unusually good for conversation. A host, shared meal and small table create high relationship production with little specialist equipment.

The weakness is customer acquisition and capacity. “Dinner with strangers” asks the room itself to justify the ticket, and ten-seat events require many repetitions to create meaningful revenue. Dinner should be offered to customers who already trust the brand or explicitly seek the format, rather than forced to carry the entire business.

The regional 35–65 dining proposition therefore becomes a depth product inside the winning model.

Vertical activity network: the venture-shaped winner#

Playtomic, SweatPals, GoodRec and StartPlaying show why vertical networks can be powerful:

  • the activity has clear supply and completion constraints;
  • participants return frequently;
  • organisers need payments, schedules, level, waivers or membership;
  • the platform can improve supply workflow before charging for discovery;
  • transaction and SaaS revenue can scale beyond company-run events.

This is harder to bootstrap as a multi-city product but may be the best venture destination after proving one activity and one city.

Multi-category discovery: important, but not first#

Liiiv and Ocio prove that “what should I do tonight?” can be productised. Liiiv monetises organisers through subscription, commission and promotion while keeping consumer access free. Ocio charges consumers for curated credits.

The model ranks below owned formats because it needs breadth, fresh inventory, attribution, partner support and repeated consumer use before any one side has strong value. The recommended company should use discovery platforms and later cross-sell selected partner activity; it should not begin by indexing the city.

Segment boundaries that matter more than interests#

  1. Availability: can the person attend the same recurring slot?
  2. Travel: will they cross the distance reliably at night?
  3. Price comfort: can the group use the same event and venue without embarrassment?
  4. Participation mode: quiet listening, active making, play, movement or open conversation?
  5. Experience level: can a beginner and an expert enjoy the same game or sport?
  6. Accessibility: mobility, hearing, sensory, dietary and language requirements.
  7. Social intent: activity, friendship, professional community or dating?
  8. Commitment: one-off trial, season, weekly team or ongoing membership?

Two people who both like sport are not useful supply if one wants competitive football and the other wants a gentle social walk.

The beachhead design#

one regional city or compact cluster
× adult good-nights-out positioning
× solo-friendly arrival
× one flagship public format
× one related small-group format
× six-week season
× existing ticketing and CRM
× no permanent venue

The regional screen places Bristol first as a learning cell, Birmingham first for non-London scale demand and Manchester first as an incumbent stress test. Those are desk-research roles rather than unconditional launch instructions; venue terms, founder access and paid intent can reverse the order.

The first season should test at least two price and capacity shapes without fragmenting into a random calendar.

Expansion order#

  1. repeat the public flagship in the same venue and weekday;
  2. prove conversion into one smaller depth format;
  3. sell a bounded season or cohort;
  4. add one corporate version of the same programme;
  5. secure recording or facilitator rights where appropriate;
  6. add a second neighbourhood or adjacent town;
  7. test a local lead who is not the founder;
  8. only then consider another city, credit wallet or third-party marketplace.

The defensible unit is no longer only a liquid dinner cell. It is a programme cell: audience, format, facilitator, venue, economics and a repeat path that another competent lead can reproduce.
