Commercial research series / 04

The Structured Social Leisure Economy

The wider category across talks, craft, games, sport, friendship and discovery, led by UK demand and tested against selected international models.

Geography
UK; selected European and US comparators
Research date
2026-09-03
Status
Internal working research

The category is larger than friendship events#

Hosted friendship, offline dating and their supporting platforms sit inside a larger commercial field.

People do not only buy “meeting people”. They buy a reason to leave home that still feels worthwhile if they make no friend, date or useful contact. A lecture, pottery class, padel match, reading party, chess night or Dungeons & Dragons table gives the customer an answer to three difficult questions before they book:

  1. What will I actually do?
  2. What will I talk about when I arrive?
  3. Will the evening have been wasted if I do not connect with anyone?

The broader opportunity is best described in this report as structured social leisure: paid or organised time outside work and home in which an activity, subject or ritual makes participation with other people easier. This is an analytical label created for the report, not an observed industry category.

It includes four starting wedges:

Starting wedge What the customer thinks they are buying Representative operators Social connection's role
Social-first A group of people to meet Dinners With Friends, Timeleft, 222 The explicit product
Content-first An idea, speaker, story or performance Seed Talks, Lectures on Tap, 5x15, The Moth Makes the room and the conversation possible
Activity-first Something to make, play, practise or learn Social Pottery, Reading Rhythms, Knightcap, GoodRec Turns strangers into participants
Access/discovery A reliable answer to “what should I do?” Liiiv, Ocio, Fever, ClassPass, Playtomic A benefit of the inventory rather than a guaranteed outcome

These models overlap, but their economics are not interchangeable. A lecture operator owns programming and audience risk. A pottery studio owns materials, firing and possibly rent. A pickup-sport service owns facility slots and team completion. A discovery app owns a two-sided marketplace problem. Calling all four “events” hides the thing that determines whether each company works.

Activity is a social prosthetic#

“Come and make friends” asks the customer to expose a need. “Come and hear why memory fails”, “learn to throw a pot” or “join Thursday's five-a-side game” lets the same person act from curiosity instead.

The activity performs six commercial jobs:

  • permission: there is a socially normal reason to enter the room alone;
  • script: people immediately know what to discuss or do;
  • sorting: the subject attracts a partly self-selected audience without a long questionnaire;
  • risk reduction: the ticket retains standalone value even without a new relationship;
  • memory: the person leaves with an idea, skill, score, story or object;
  • recurrence: a series, season, league, course or practice gives a reason to meet again.

This explains why the category can be larger than explicit friendship search. It also changes the acquisition message. The activity should usually lead; belonging should be experienced and then retained.

International comparators test mechanisms, not UK demand#

The recommendation in this report is grounded in UK search, UK prices, UK operators and UK launch conditions. Selected European and North American companies enter the analysis only when they expose a transferable mechanism:

  • Liiiv shows how a free consumer discovery layer can combine organiser subscriptions, ticket commission, paid promotion and external redirects. Liiiv terms
  • Reading Rhythms shows a host playbook, local ticket share, tiered membership and brand-funded chapter network. Reading Rhythms partnerships
  • GoodRec shows the operational value of completing a game with the right player count, host and facility rather than merely listing sport. GoodRec
  • The Offline Club shows a licensed city-leader model in which local operators retain a majority of ticket, sponsorship, partnership, merchandise and optional B2B or membership earnings. City-leader model

None proves that the same customer acquisition, price, retention or city economics will transfer to Britain. They are design evidence, not UK market-size evidence.

Two kinds of value exist in the same room#

Every format can be judged on two axes:

standalone activity value: would I pay even if I spoke to nobody?
relationship production: does the format make repeated human connection likely?

This creates four useful quadrants:

Lower relationship production Higher relationship production
Higher standalone value theatre, large lecture, concert course, small workshop, team sport, TTRPG campaign
Lower standalone value generic mixer, unhosted networking well-hosted friendship dinner or recurring salon

Large lectures can acquire efficiently because 100 people consume one speaker, but audience members may never meet. A six-person D&D campaign has extraordinary repeated interaction but is capped by facilitator time. A pottery workshop has an object and a skill as fallback value but incurs materials and firing. Dinner needs little equipment but places nearly all value on the room.

The optimal operator does not have to choose one quadrant forever. It can use a high-standalone-value public format to acquire and a high-relationship-production cohort to retain.

The market definition#

The market is not the sum of event ticket sales. It is a stack of expenditures that can be reorganised around participation:

  • leisure tickets;
  • classes and courses;
  • sports facility and game fees;
  • food and drink around organised activity;
  • memberships and credit subscriptions;
  • corporate team and employee-benefit budgets;
  • brand experiential and sampling budgets;
  • venue customer-acquisition and off-peak utilisation budgets;
  • creator, instructor and host software;
  • media, recordings, professional education and IP.

The same evening can touch several layers, but an operator must not claim them all as its revenue. Restaurant spend belongs to the restaurant unless the operator owns the transaction. Sponsor reach is not ticket revenue. Marketplace gross booking value is not commission revenue. Prepaid credits are a delivery obligation until the accounting treatment is resolved.

Strategic implication#

Attendance, hosting and local liquidity are hard assets, but friendship dining should not automatically be the initial acquisition wedge.

The founder conclusion is:

Start with a format people are proud to buy for its own sake. Design it so arriving alone is normal. Use smaller repeated formats to turn attendance into belonging.

Dinner remains powerful, especially for older regional customers and relationship depth. It is now one format in the system rather than the default front door.

This publication distinguishes observed evidence, derived calculations, commercial inference and unknowns.
