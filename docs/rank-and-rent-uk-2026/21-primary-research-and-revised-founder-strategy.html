Primary Research and the Revised Founder Strategy

A multi-workstream buyer and consumer research programme, ninety-day validation design and the final founder recommendation.

Geography
United Kingdom
Research date
2026-09-03
Status
Published research

What remains unknown#

The deeper secondary work establishes search auctions, competitor models, filed company scale, public cost anchors and plausible buyer segments. It does not establish:

  • a contractor's actual gross contribution by job type;
  • the accepted definition they will sign;
  • source-level close rates;
  • cancellation, duplicate and credit rates;
  • response capacity by hour and postcode;
  • the price at which a buyer retains;
  • organic click and AI-assistance behaviour for the proposed site;
  • attainable ranking cost or time;
  • consumer trust in a transparent referral brand.

These are primary-research questions. More keyword rows cannot answer them.

Workstream 1: 40 buyer interviews#

Run ten interviews in each priority cell: Manchester fire, Manchester asbestos, Bristol drainage and Edinburgh boilers. Recruit a mix of owner-operators, regional firms, multi-branch operators and one acquisition intermediary where relevant.

Screener#

Confirm active service, postcode coverage, last-month capacity, acquisition channels, authority over marketing spend and willingness to discuss ranges. Exclude firms that do not currently provide the service or cannot legally or operationally fulfil it.

Interview guide#

Ask for the last 20–50 enquiries, not an abstract “good lead”:

  1. Where did each source come from?
  2. What percentage was contactable, serviceable and quoted?
  3. How many were won, lost or remain open?
  4. What was the job-value and gross-contribution range?
  5. How quickly was first contact attempted?
  6. What creates a legitimate credit?
  7. Which postcodes and job types are unwanted?
  8. What capacity exists next week and next month?
  9. Which directories, agencies or ads are currently paid for?
  10. What reporting would make a pilot credible?

End with a concrete pilot card: unit, definition, expected range, cap, data fields, price hypothesis, reporting and stop date. Interest without acceptance of a pilot card is not demand validation.

Evidence standard#

Record interview date, role, company identity, geography, notes, confidence and whether claims were supported by CRM, invoice or diary evidence. Publish aggregates and anonymised ranges only where appropriate. Do not publish sensitive buyer economics without permission.

Workstream 2: mystery-shop the alternatives#

Create a lawful research matrix for each cell:

  • platform or agency;
  • price and billable event;
  • geography and category availability;
  • verification requirements;
  • lead-sharing or exclusivity;
  • refunds and credits;
  • contract term and cancellation;
  • reporting and integrations;
  • consumer disclosure;
  • response experience.

Use public pages and sales conversations in an honest research capacity. Do not submit fabricated homeowner projects that waste providers' time. Request demonstrations or terms as a prospective buyer only where there is genuine potential use.

The goal is not a feature checklist. It is to identify the buyer's next-best use of budget.

Workstream 3: 80 consumer problem interviews#

Run 20 recent-customer interviews per priority cell. Recruit people who commissioned or attempted to commission the service within six months.

Explore:

  • trigger and urgency;
  • first channel used;
  • language and uncertainty;
  • trust checks;
  • pricing expectations;
  • number of providers contacted;
  • reasons for abandonment;
  • what information was missing;
  • AI, search, map and marketplace use;
  • willingness to complete a structured brief;
  • outcome and retrospective advice.

The output is a journey and information model, not testimonial copy.

Workstream 4: price and response census#

For each cell, define a standard hypothetical property context and request public or permissioned quotes from a documented sample. Record:

  • provider legal identity and service area;
  • date and channel;
  • response time;
  • availability window;
  • quote type and inclusions;
  • price range and VAT status;
  • credential evidence;
  • follow-up behaviour.

Do not misrepresent a real booking intention. Many providers will give general ranges or participate in a stated industry survey. Publish the method, sample, missingness and limitations.

This creates the original local data that generic affiliate content lacks.

Workstream 5: small paid-intent experiments#

Paid validation is expensive in these cells. Use exact and phrase match around tightly defined commercial queries, negative keywords, city targeting and capped hours only after a buyer agrees the route. Treat the campaign as a bounded experiment with an explicit budget, owner and stop condition.

The experiment measures:

  • impressions and impression share;
  • observed search terms;
  • landing conversion;
  • valid contact and acceptance;
  • connected call or appointment;
  • buyer outcome;
  • direct contribution.

Do not optimise to cheap form submissions. Optimise the learning toward accepted and won cohorts.

Workstream 6: organic and AI evidence baseline#

Before content scale, define 50–100 queries per cell across:

  • urgent action;
  • quote and booking;
  • price;
  • eligibility and regulation;
  • problem diagnosis;
  • provider comparison;
  • local building or property context;
  • brand navigation.

Monthly, record conventional results, local pack, Local Services Ads, AI feature presence, cited domains, the site's visibility and eventual assisted conversions. This is a research panel, not a ranking guarantee.

Ninety-day validation design#

Days 1–21: buyer and consumer evidence#

  • complete 20 buyer interviews across the top two cells;
  • complete 20 consumer interviews;
  • resolve buyer identities and credentials;
  • obtain at least two signed pilot cards;
  • choose one cell.

Days 22–45: build the minimum acquisition utility#

  • publish five to eight genuinely useful decision assets;
  • implement a transparent owner and referral disclosure;
  • configure call/form attribution;
  • version qualification and credit rules;
  • create the buyer outcome report;
  • capture the first price and availability sample.

Days 46–75: controlled acquisition#

  • activate one capped distribution source;
  • manually qualify every event;
  • review calls and forms daily;
  • reconcile buyer outcomes weekly;
  • interview accepted and rejected consumers where permitted;
  • improve the brief and content from observed failure.

Days 76–90: price and continuation decision#

  • mature the cohort as far as the service cycle permits;
  • calculate accepted-event contribution;
  • compare buyer cost per win with alternatives;
  • settle credits;
  • obtain a paid continuation or stop;
  • publish an internal evidence memo for the next capital gate.

The revised founder strategy#

Positioning#

Do not sell “rank-and-rent websites.” Sell exclusive, auditable local opportunities with better context and clearer economics.

The consumer proposition is equally direct: understand the job, prepare the right facts and reach an appropriate available provider without pretending the publisher is that provider.

First product#

Choose between Manchester fire-risk assessment and Bristol drainage after interviews.

  • Manchester fire offers higher CPC evidence and richer qualification defensibility, but slower B2B outcomes and professional-boundary risk.
  • Bristol drainage offers faster call outcomes, larger local demand and simpler validation, but lower job values and strong answer-rate sensitivity.

Manchester asbestos remains a strong evidence cell but the public survey-price anchor makes paid acquisition difficult. Edinburgh boilers can support larger appointment economics but needs deeper property and sales qualification.

Commercial model#

Start with capped qualified calls or exclusive accepted leads. Add a small attribution/reporting base only when the buyer uses it. Move to base-plus-performance after 90 days of outcome data. Do not sign an annual fixed territory tenancy from estimated search volume.

Content model#

Publish fewer pages and more evidence:

  • original price and availability samples;
  • regulator-grounded decision tools;
  • provider credential and service-area records;
  • local response and outcome data;
  • detailed real cases;
  • transparent method and correction history.

Every city page must earn its existence through unique local facts and fulfilment.

Distribution model#

Organic search is the initial asymmetric channel, not the only channel. Build:

  • genuine partner referrals;
  • repeat and brand navigation;
  • email or reminder utility where the service permits;
  • structured feeds for buyers or agencies;
  • selective paid validation;
  • marketplace affiliate fallback where economics and disclosure work.

Capital model#

Bootstrap the evidence lane. Reinvest contribution after retention. Hire for qualification and buyer success before multiplying content. Consider outside capital only after three repeatable cells reveal a network or workflow opportunity that capital can accelerate.

Final investment decision#

The opportunity is real, but the old promise is wrong.

There is observable UK search demand, material advertiser pressure, explicit buyer-acquisition demand, large filed platform revenues and many active revenue models. There is also direct competition from Google, uneven platform profitability, expensive paid validation, strict profile and consumer-protection constraints, and no audited representative distribution of UK rank-and-rent outcomes.

The recommended investment is therefore a single transparent market-cell experiment, budgeted as primary research. Advance only when a real buyer pays, retains and shares enough outcome data to establish contribution. The compounding asset is the evidence, routing and buyer network around the cell. The domain is one interface to it.

If the operator is unwilling to interview buyers, handle disputes, maintain provider evidence and measure outcomes, do not enter this market. If it is willing, the opportunity is larger—and much more operational—than renting pages.
