Validation and Twelve-Month Launch Plan

A buyer-first 90-day validation sprint, measurement gates and a controlled twelve-month rollout.

Geography
United Kingdom
Research date
2026-09-03
Status
Published research

Recommended starting portfolio#

Run commercial discovery across four evidence-backed lanes:

  1. Asbestos surveys in Manchester for the strongest explicit/local CPC agreement.
  2. Fire risk assessment in Manchester for premises-qualified B2B appointments.
  3. Drain unblocking in Bristol for stable local demand and fast call-funnel feedback.
  4. Boiler replacement in Edinburgh for high-order-value quote economics.

Then select at most two validation lanes. Fire-risk assessment and drainage provide the clearest contrast between considered B2B appointments and urgent consumer calls. Asbestos remains partner-led because competence and survey economics must be established first; boilers require verified Gas Safe buyer capacity. Do not launch four sites.

Commercial cleaning in Edinburgh is paused until a one-month search spike and explicit/local disagreement are resolved.

Days 1–15: validate buyers#

For each candidate market, complete a short commercial screen; for the one or two selected lanes:

  • map 20 providers;
  • verify coverage, credentials and visible trust;
  • conduct ten commercial interviews;
  • obtain two written trial commitments;
  • define one accepted-lead or appointment specification;
  • agree answer-time, capacity, duplicate and credit rules;
  • collect buyer contribution bands and current acquisition alternatives.

Gate: no build beyond a prototype unless two credible buyers agree to the same event definition and outcome feedback.

Days 16–30: build the transaction#

Build one focused service experience per market:

  • transparent intermediary identity;
  • category qualification flow;
  • provider-selection and credential standard;
  • clear handoff and privacy notice;
  • call or form tracking;
  • append-only lead states;
  • rejection and credit workflow;
  • one decision page with real evidence.

Do not create a city directory. The first site proves one valuable transaction.

Days 31–60: acquisition proof#

Acquire a bounded cohort from several sources:

  • partner referrals and existing provider traffic;
  • original local data or tools;
  • useful local citations and relationships;
  • tightly scoped content around direct and hybrid intent;
  • marketplace or affiliate fallback where approved;
  • a small paid validation only when break-even CPC supports it.

Any paid test should have a named owner, explicit budget, narrow targeting and predefined stop conditions.

Measure:

  • impressions and landings by page and intent;
  • raw contacts and connection;
  • qualification and acceptance;
  • buyer response time;
  • appointment, quote and win;
  • rejected, duplicate, spam and credited contacts;
  • revenue per accepted lead;
  • complaints and privacy requests.

Days 61–90: prove pricing#

Continue with per-accepted-event billing. Review the cohort weekly with both buyers.

At day 90 calculate:

acceptance rate = accepted ÷ raw contacts
buyer close rate = won jobs ÷ accepted leads
publisher revenue per raw lead = lead revenue ÷ raw leads
buyer contribution per lead = buyer job contribution ÷ accepted leads
credit rate = credited ÷ billed leads

Go: repeated accepted leads, at least two active buyers, positive delivery contribution, reliable response and no material compliance issue.

Revise: contacts exist but query quality, buyer response or qualification is weak.

Stop: no buyer market, negative evidence-based economics, deceptive presentation required, or visibility depends on an ineligible profile.

Months 4–6: deepen the vertical#

Fire assessment can add premises-type tools and a second city once qualified provider coverage exists. Drainage can add symptom and property-responsibility tools only after response and contribution are proven. Boiler content should expand by genuine system and property decisions rather than interchangeable city pages.

Potential replication cities are Leeds or Birmingham for fire-risk assessment and other locally screened cities for the selected trade. These are hypotheses from the broad screen, not pre-approved builds. Repeat buyer, local-geo and result validation in each place. Cleaning cannot replicate until its base demand has been re-established.

Begin publishing original aggregate evidence where privacy and sample size permit:

  • response-time distributions;
  • common quote drivers;
  • acceptance and rejection reasons;
  • anonymised price bands;
  • provider-coverage gaps;
  • turnaround by premises or job type.

Months 7–9: mature the contract#

Move stable buyers to a base-plus-performance agreement. Preserve:

  • accepted-event definition;
  • performance fee;
  • minimum base and included volume;
  • caps and overflow;
  • payment and credit timing;
  • profile and website ownership;
  • content approval for technical claims;
  • data protection responsibilities;
  • termination and routing transition.

Add a third buyer where possible. A buyer bench protects the user from missed service and the publisher from concentration.

Months 10–12: choose the company shape#

Use measured economics to choose among:

  • specialist lead publisher;
  • vertical marketplace with provider availability;
  • licensed tenant platform;
  • owned operator or joint venture.

Expand to London only if buyer contribution and conversion can withstand its auction and result competition. Perform borough and postcode research first.

Year-one budget logic#

Budget by gated evidence, not a blanket number:

  1. buyer research and compliance design;
  2. one transaction prototype;
  3. instrumentation and routing;
  4. a bounded acquisition cohort;
  5. original evidence and authority;
  6. second city only after positive accepted-lead contribution.

The FatRank case's £83,000 reported cost proves that serious competition can absorb significant capital. The small promoter cases prove only that cheaper outcomes are claimed. Neither should set this project's budget.

Year-one target shape#

A credible year-one business has:

  • one or two verticals;
  • six to twelve evidence-rich territories;
  • two or more buyers per active market;
  • accepted-lead and outcome data;
  • no speculative Business Profiles;
  • a hybrid contract in mature cohorts;
  • original local operating evidence;
  • positive contribution before founder labour in the best territories;
  • explicit closures for failed markets.

Hundreds of thin sites are not a milestone.

Immediate next decisions#

  1. Conduct initial buyer screens across Manchester fire and asbestos, Bristol drainage and Edinburgh boilers; take the best one or two into ten-interview validation.
  2. Validate target-postcode result composition manually for the selected cities and devices.
  3. Turn the completed priority keyword expansion into a non-overlapping landing and negative-keyword map for any controlled validation.
  4. Obtain a legal and privacy review of the actual quote and call flow.
  5. Build one qualification prototype per niche.
  6. Set paid acquisition ceilings from buyer contribution rather than observed CPC.
  7. Make the first scale decision from accepted leads, not rankings.

The deeper desk research is complete enough to choose and challenge the interviews. It is not a substitute for them.
