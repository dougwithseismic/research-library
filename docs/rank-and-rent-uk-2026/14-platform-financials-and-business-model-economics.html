Platform Financials and Business-Model Economics

Seven filed operator comparisons, revenue trajectories, margins, capital signals and strict comparability limits.

What the filings add#

The first report used three filed operators to establish that the category contains material companies. The expanded comparison adds Yell, the Federation of Master Builders, Airtasker UK and Houzz UK, and compares the latest disclosed period with the preceding period where possible.

The normalised financial table keeps turnover, operating result, employees, balance-sheet facts and disclosure limitations separate. It does not rank “best” businesses. Periods, group boundaries, accounting policies and products differ.

The revenue trajectory matters more than a single total#

Yell: the directory becomes a service suite#

Yell's turnover fell from £96.026 million to £90.923 million in the year to March 2026. Within that:

  • Yell-platform revenue fell from £41.3 million to £28.6 million;
  • digital-marketing-services revenue fell from £41.7 million to £30.7 million;
  • Solutions revenue rose from £13.0 million to £31.6 million;
  • average revenue per customer rose from £1,683 to £1,873;
  • operating profit fell from £3.214 million to £0.981 million;
  • average employees fell from 120 to 105.

The company says revenue includes directory listing subscriptions, websites, paid media, SEO and Solutions. Yell filing history The strategic inference is not that directories are dead. It is that a mature local audience alone does not freeze the product. Packaging and revenue mix move toward more managed and integrated services.

Bark: scale without current operating profit#

Bark's revenue declined from £84.927 million in 2024 to £71.603 million in 2025. It moved from a £4.718 million operating profit to a £0.858 million operating loss, while average headcount fell from 213 to 172.

The geographic split is useful: UK revenue was £29.573 million; North America £28.748 million; Australia £6.515 million; Europe £4.081 million; and the rest of the world £2.686 million. The filing describes lead and subscription revenue. Bark filing history

This is the clearest warning against equating a high CPC, a lead fee and gross margin. A marketplace can have tens of millions in revenue and still lose money in a period. Acquisition, qualification, sales, refunds, support, technology and brand are real expenses.

MyBuilder: a focused marketplace can produce attractive disclosed margins#

MyBuilder increased revenue from £24.698 million to £25.015 million and reported £5.669 million operating profit, a 22.7% margin, down from £6.472 million. Average employment was 88. Its terms and accounts describe a shortlist-fee marketplace. MyBuilder filing history

The result shows that category focus and a clear billable event can support profitability. It does not tell us the economics of a new independent site, the outcome of an average tradesperson or the cost to replace traffic.

FMB: acquisition sits inside a trust product#

FMB reported £5.399 million turnover and £4.251 million membership income in 2024, with a £0.563 million operating loss. It reported 6,635 members, 174 fewer than a year earlier, and 89% retention. Its current membership page states £62.69 plus VAT per month, with a first-year inspection fee of £14.85 plus VAT per month. FMB membership

This is not a marketplace pure play. Members buy accreditation, representation and services as well as consumer discovery. The lesson is that lead volume is not the only retention mechanism. A product that makes the provider more credible and operationally capable can survive a weak month of enquiries better than an opaque lead fee.

What omitted accounts still reveal#

Airtasker UK filed small-company accounts without turnover or a profit and loss account. It disclosed £399,848 cash, net liabilities of £5.558 million, accumulated losses of £18.873 million and two average employees for the year to June 2025. The accounts describe a November 2024 financing package of £4 million in Channel 4 media inventory and £2 million of parent cash through convertible notes. Airtasker UK filing history

The capital structure demonstrates the cost of trying to create two-sided marketplace liquidity and consumer awareness. The UK company is part of a wider group, so its balance sheet cannot be read as the whole Airtasker business or as evidence that its marketplace lacks users.

Houzz UK also omitted turnover. It reported £214,856 profit after tax, £3.637 million net assets, £156,011 cash and zero average employees. Houzz UK filing history Zero employees may reflect group or service-company structure. It does not mean the UK product has no activity.

Four strategic conclusions#

First, revenue model matters. MyBuilder's shortlist event, Vetted's memberships, Bark's contact credits, Yell's services and Airtasker's transaction fees create different costs and risk allocation.

Second, scale does not remove traffic or operating risk. Bark and Yell both reported falling turnover in their latest periods despite strong brands.

Third, workflow and trust expand the revenue base. Houzz sells software and promotion. FMB sells accreditation and member services. Yell bundles managed acquisition. These adjacencies reduce dependence on one stream but can also add labour and complexity.

Fourth, an independent portfolio should be contribution-led from the first cell. It should know revenue per accepted event, variable qualification cost, routing cost, credits, call expense, buyer churn and founder time. “Organic traffic is free” is not an accounting policy.

What the filings cannot answer#

They do not reveal:

  • average lead price by category;
  • consumer-to-lead or lead-to-job conversion;
  • buyer gross contribution;
  • refund and credit rates;
  • paid versus organic acquisition mix;
  • local liquidity;
  • cohort retention by buyer size;
  • a new entrant's attainable margin.

Those unknowns define the primary research programme. Companies House is registry evidence supplied by companies; it is not independently verified market research and does not prove current trading quality or buying intent.

This publication distinguishes observed evidence, derived calculations, commercial inference and unknowns.
