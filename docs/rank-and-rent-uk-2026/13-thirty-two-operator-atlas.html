Thirty-Two Operators and What They Reveal

Search, directories, marketplaces, lead suppliers, agencies, trust schemes and the enabling software layer.

Geography
United Kingdom
Research date
2026-09-03
Status
Published research

Why the operator set had to expand#

The initial report treated a few large directories as proof that local acquisition is commercially real. That was directionally correct but strategically incomplete. A founder needs to see the substitutes available to both sides of the market.

The expanded operator atlas is purposive rather than exhaustive. It includes 32 UK-relevant products across search, directories, marketplaces, direct lead supply, agencies, trade bodies, operator education and infrastructure. It records the public commercial unit and verification claim without treating first-party success statements as independently proven.

The category has not converged on one way to charge. That is a finding. It means the correct unit follows the customer's workflow: a connected call for an emergency, a shortlist for a considered quote, an attended appointment for a field-sales team, a monthly area membership for ongoing work, or a take rate where the platform controls booking and payment.

Direct lead and appointment suppliers#

The second competitive set rarely appears in “best trades websites” lists, but it competes directly for contractor acquisition budgets.

Watts Marketing describes exclusive, qualified, booked appointments across windows, solar, heat pumps, boilers, insulation and conservatory roofs. It explicitly distinguishes an appointment from warm data. Watts This is a better commercial comparator for an Edinburgh boiler proposition than a generic directory membership.

Trade Leads UK publishes a tariff “from £19” per human-verified construction lead, with a monthly option. Trade Leads UK The number is an observable offer, not a universal market clearing price. Its lead definition, job values and source mix differ from an exclusive emergency call.

Exclusive Home Leads, Sales Fuel, LeadZen, ElevateOne, Strive Online and BuildBright all sell some combination of exclusivity, qualification, geographic protection, appointment setting and follow-up. Their public outcome claims remain marketing evidence until a buyer supplies invoices and cohort results. Collectively, however, they show what buyers are promised:

  • one buyer or one buyer per area;
  • a confirmed service and location;
  • budget or timescale qualification;
  • calendar booking rather than a raw form;
  • automated speed-to-lead;
  • replacement or guarantee language;
  • an acquisition system rather than a domain lease.

The independent publisher has to match this service standard or be cheaper for a reason the buyer understands.

Trust institutions and trade bodies#

FMB, Which Trusted Traders and TrustMark reveal another value pool. A homeowner is not only selecting availability; they are reducing the risk of letting someone into a property or commissioning regulated work.

The FMB filing reports £4.25 million of membership income and 6,635 members for 2024, alongside 89% stated retention. Its product includes inspection, accreditation and member services, so the figures are not a lead-generation revenue estimate. They do show that UK builders pay recurring money for a bundle of trust, identity and opportunity.

This suggests a stronger page than a thin “best X in Y” list. A defensible property-service request can show:

  • the legal provider identity;
  • relevant scheme membership and expiry;
  • insurance evidence where appropriate;
  • exactly who verifies each fact;
  • review source, date and moderation rules;
  • service radius and capacity;
  • what happens if the provider is unavailable or unsuitable.

Publishing those facts creates maintenance work. That maintenance is part of the product.

The infrastructure layer#

CallRail's observed UK plans start at £60 per month and increase to £220 before usage. CallRail UK pricing It sells call and form attribution, not demand. LeadByte documents delivery rules based on buyer contracts, budgets, prices and logic. LeadByte distribution WhatConverts also sells cross-channel lead attribution and qualification.

These tools expose hidden costs in the passive-income story:

  • phone numbers, minutes, recordings and compliance;
  • duplicate detection and source attribution;
  • routing retries and buyer caps;
  • data retention and access requests;
  • buyer credit balances and refunds;
  • outcome reconciliation;
  • alerting when delivery fails.

A spreadsheet and forwarded phone number can validate one buyer. It cannot safely support a multi-city portfolio.

Search-and-agency terms in the same screen carry a higher weighted CPC than the broad marketplace group. Yell business returned a £16.70 average CPC, while Google Guaranteed returned £6.21. Small individual rows should not be over-read, but they reinforce that supplier acquisition and verification products attract commercial auctions.

Five models hiding under one label#

The atlas reveals five structurally different companies:

  1. Media owner: attracts an audience and sells placement, affiliate events or territory access.
  2. Lead exchange: captures requests and sells contact or qualification events.
  3. Managed acquisition partner: runs ads, content, qualification and follow-up for a protected buyer.
  4. Workflow platform: embeds in quoting, reviews, routing and payments.
  5. Service operator: fulfils or controls the job and owns the customer economics.

Calling all five “rank and rent” makes strategy worse. They require different capital, capability, compliance, margins and exit buyers.

White space#

The crowded horizontal directory is not the attractive gap. The gap is a narrow, evidence-heavy market cell that combines:

  • high-value or urgent consumer intent;
  • a formal lead or appointment definition;
  • verified suppliers with live capacity;
  • original local or category data;
  • transparent routing and outcome measurement;
  • a buyer contract that rewards quality;
  • an interface designed for calls and structured briefs, not page views.

That is closer to a vertical acquisition utility than a rented SEO site.
